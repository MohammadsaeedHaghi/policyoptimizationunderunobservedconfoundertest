Three details taken from the authors' code, not the paper text. We ported from CausalML/confounding-robust-policy-improvement (data_scenarios.py), which resolves ambiguities that would otherwise change the experiment: (1) their Γ is on the LOG scale — the code passes Γ = 1.5 into a routine that exponentiates it, so the true odds ratio is Λ* = e1.5 = 4.4817, and that (not 1.5) is the matched Γ we flag; (2) the outcome noise is 2·N(0,1), not N(0,1) as the caption says; (3) the ξ level-shift coefficient is 1.5, not the caption's ω = 1. We verified the realized odds ratio equals Λ* to 10-12 on both branches.
Disclosed reduction: 5-vector to scalar. Their policy class is linear over X ∈ R5; our estimators are univariate, so every method — ours and the baselines alike — sees the SAME scalar index. We run both natural choices and report both: main arm = the nominal-propensity index x = θ'X5/4, and the CATE-index arm x = βtreat'X5/8. The propensity index is the headline because the paper's phenomenon — unconfoundedness-assuming methods DO HARM — survives the reduction there (naive gives back 0.168 of the 0.308 available gain); on the CATE index the naive policy is already within 0.024 of the oracle, so that arm is reported as a DO-NO-HARM check rather than as a win condition. Nothing else about the DGP is touched.

corr(x, S) = -0.366 (main) and -0.708 (CATE index) -- both far from the zero-coupling anchor of the KMZ benchmark, so the Wasserstein term has real signal to use.

Reward = -loss. The oracle treats where CATE > 0: 0.400 of units (main arm).

Doctors treat the treatment-favorable, so the observed contrast sits ABOVE the truth and crosses zero too far right: the naive policy treats 0.667 of units where the oracle treats 0.400, and gives back 0.168 of the 0.308 available between never-treat and oracle.

Declared expectation (written before the wave). This is the X-TRACKABLE corner of our diagnostic map: corr(x, S) = -0.366 on the main arm (versus ≈ 0 on the KMZ'19 benchmark). The prediction is therefore that the Wasserstein term HELPS here — O-W should beat box-only O-X and beat the naive policy it is correcting, and the sharp box should NOT, because sharpness tightens the same one-dimensional bound the box already has without ever using X-structure.

Verdict (generated from the landed numbers). The declared prediction FAILS on this benchmark, and we report it as measured. The Wasserstein term does not rescue the paper's confounding: IPW-O-W reaches -1.885 against box-only IPW-O-X -1.807 (-0.078), the infinite-data naive policy -1.610 (-0.275) and Sharp-O-X -2.063 (+0.178), on an oracle of -1.442 and a never-treat floor of -1.751. Doubly-robust behaves the same way (DR-O-W -1.782 vs DR-O-X -1.782). Every robust variant except Hajek-O-X (-1.751) lands BELOW the never-treat floor of -1.751, and the best cell anywhere on the L × Γ surface (IPW-O-X, -1.703) is still short of the naive policy. The comparison methods do not do this: Kallus (-1.752) and Sharp-O-X (-2.063) both hold the never-treat line rather than falling through it.

Diagnosis, stated plainly. This is not a small-sample artifact — the same run at n = 400 is no better (IPW-O-W -1.943), so doubling the data does not close it. It is the objective. Our solvers maximise worst-case value, minW V̂W(π), scoring each candidate against its own private adversary; Kallus-Zhou minimise worst-case regret against a baseline, maxW[V̂W(π0) - V̂W(π)], where one and the same W scores both terms. In regret form π = π0 scores exactly 0, so nothing returned can be certified worse than the baseline — a floor our value form structurally lacks. On this DGP (outcome sd 4.36 against CATE sd 2.79, true IPW weights reaching 168) that gap is wide enough to swallow the experiment: the never-treat baseline is scored by an adversary free to make control outcomes look terrible, so treating policies win the objective and lose the truth.

What this does and does not say. It does not overturn the coupling diagnostic — corr(x, S) = -0.366 here and the transport term does behave differently than on the zero-coupling benchmark (a wider budget, cε = 2.0, moves IPW-O-W from -1.885 to -1.847). What it says is that coupling alone is not sufficient: when the estimator's variance and the absence of a baseline floor dominate, X-structure has nothing to work with. The honest conclusion is that this benchmark demands the regret-form variant of O-W — same uncertainty set, same Wasserstein balance constraint, objective rewritten against a baseline — and that our current claims should be scoped to the value form until that exists.
