Robust policy optimization under unobserved confounding: two complementary stress tests

When can the Wasserstein-balanced robust methods (the O-W family) be trusted? We show two discrete-X, binary-treatment DGPs that each break a different naive baseline — and that the O-W methods are the only ones that survive both.

All methods estimate the propensity from \((X,T)\); the hidden confounder \(S\) is never used. Methods follow the estimator–uncertaintyset–W naming.

Test 1 — exp_owgap: unobserved-S confounding breaks naive IPW

Story. Fitness \(X\) (7 levels), hidden vitality \(S\) with \(P(S{=}{+}1\mid X)=\sigma(10X)\) (S tracks X). Selection \(e=\sigma(0.8S-2X)\); outcomes \(\mu_0=8S,\ \mu_1=9S+1.5X\) — vitality dominates both arms (huge model bias), the therapy effect is subtle (\(\mathrm{CATE}=E[S\mid X]+1.5X\), treat iff \(X\gt 0\)). Naive IPW can't see the hidden-S confounding; the Wasserstein balance reaches \(S\) through its correlation with \(X\).

Test 2 — exp_nmcap: a non-monotone effect breaks naive AIPW under the cap

Story. Severity \(X\), hidden robustness \(S\perp X\). The therapy helps a middle band \(X^2\lt 0.3\) and harms the severe edges (\(\mathrm{CATE}=3(0.3-X^2)\)). Clinicians escalate the robust \(S{=}{+}1\) and the extreme-severity edges, so the treated edges are a high-\(S\) elite whose outcomes look great. The naive outcome model is fooled into crediting the edges; under cap \(\le 50\%\) the naive doubly-robust method spends its budget on the harmful edges, while the robust O / O-W methods recover the middle band.

Naive IPW self-corrects here (the edge over-selection shows up in the X-propensity), so it ties O-W — which is why the pair of DGPs matters: no single naive baseline is safe across confounding structures.

Over-conservatism — what happens at extreme Γ and ε

The robust set \(U(\Gamma,\epsilon)\) is supposed to cover the plausible amount of hidden confounding. Push \(\Gamma\) and the Wasserstein radius \(\epsilon\) to unrealistically large values and the worst-case treated value drops below control everywhere, so the value-maximiser hedges to never-treat and the realised value collapses (owgap, uncapped). Two lessons:

  • The pure-IPW methods collapse (IPW-O-W, IPW-O-X) — from ~oracle 0.85 down to ~0.08 (never-treat) at \(c_\epsilon=16,\ \Gamma=1000\); monotone in both knobs.
  • The doubly-robust methods do NOT collapse — only the IPW-correction term sees the worst case, so as the set explodes they fall back to the (biased) outcome-model policy (~0.6) rather than to never-treat. Hajek-O-X (regret) collapses earliest and hardest, to exactly never-treat, by its do-no-harm fallback.

Takeaway: \(c_\epsilon\!\approx\!1\) (the tight, calibrated radius) is the right operating point; cranking \(\Gamma\) and \(\epsilon\) together is assuming-the-impossible and throws away all value.

Honest caveats.
  • Under a capacity cap, the cap rescues whichever naive correction is well-specified (propensity in nmcap, outcome model in owgap), so beating every naive baseline capped is structurally hard. The O-W methods match the best and beat the failing one in every case; the cleanest uniform wins are uncapped.
  • The X-X family is Γ-free (flat). The value-objective robust methods keep a bounded worst case, so they soften rather than collapse with Γ; only the regret method Hajek-O-X collapses to never-treat.
  • All claims are paired across seeds (same data draw) with bootstrap CIs — the right test for "does O-W beat the baseline".
