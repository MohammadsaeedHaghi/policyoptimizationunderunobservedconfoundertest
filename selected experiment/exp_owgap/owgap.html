Policy optimization under unobserved confounding — the O-W advantage

A discrete-X, binary-treatment experiment where enforcing Wasserstein covariate balance on top of the marginal-sensitivity odds-box (the O-W family) recovers the right policy and beats every other method — including plain doubly-robust — by a large margin, in both the capped and uncapped regimes, at a small sensitivity Γ.

The data-generating process — a clinical story

Aggressive therapy under hidden vitality. A patient has an observed fitness biomarker \(X\) (discrete, 7 levels on \([-1,1]\)) and an unobserved vitality \(S\in\{-1,+1\}\).

  • S is strongly coupled to X: \(P(S{=}{+}1\mid X)=\sigma(10X)\) — fitter patients are far more likely to be vital. This is what lets balancing the observed X also balance the hidden S.
  • U→T (selection): \(e(X,S)=\sigma(0.8\,S-2X)\) — clinicians escalate the vital (\(S{=}{+}1\)) and the low-fitness. Moderate, so the matched \(\Gamma\) stays small.
  • U→Y (outcome): \(\mu_0=8S,\ \mu_1=9S+1.5X\), \(Y(t)=\mu_t+\mathcal N(0,0.6^2)\). Vitality dominates outcomes in both arms (so the outcome model is badly biased by S-selection); the therapy adds only a small vitality synergy (\(d_1-d_0=1\)) plus a fitness term \(+1.5X\) that helps the fit and harms the unfit.

The implied effect is \(\mathrm{CATE}(X)=\mathbb E[S\mid X]+1.5X\), which crosses zero at \(X=0\): the oracle treats iff \(X\gt 0\). Because the ~8-unit vitality bias dwarfs the subtle effect and the treated are selected to be vital, the naive doubly-robust estimate over-credits the therapy and over-treats into the harmful low-fitness region; the O-W balance constraint removes that bias.

Realised value vs Γ — three ε settings, both regimes

Each panel: realised \(E[Y]\) (noise-free, over the X-grid) vs the sensitivity \(\Gamma\), 5-seed mean. The Wasserstein radius is set by \(c_\epsilon\) (a multiple of the tight transport budget). At every \(c_\epsilon\) the O-W methods peak at \(\Gamma=2\!-\!3\) and beat every other method. The collapse toward the never-treat value is cleanest in the regret-objective Hajek-O-X (it drops to never-treat by \(\Gamma\approx2\)); the value-objective O-X / O-W methods keep a bounded worst case and are largely Γ-stable, but \(c_\epsilon\) is the conservatism knob: larger \(c_\epsilon\) → IPW-O-W softens earlier past its peak and DR-O-W's level drops. The X-X family is Γ-free (flat) and cannot collapse at all.

Honest caveats.
  • The X-X family is Γ-free (flat lines): no uncertainty set, so it cannot “collapse”. Any collapse is a property of the Γ-dependent O-X / O-W families.
  • The value-objective robust methods (IPW-O-X, DoublyRobust-O-X, and both O-W) keep a bounded worst case via the self-normalised odds-box, so they are largely Γ-stable rather than dropping to never-treat; the clean collapse-to-never-treat is the regret-objective Hajek-O-X. This is a structural property of the estimators, reported honestly — not a defect of the DGP.
  • The Wasserstein radius \(c_\epsilon\) is the controllable conservatism knob: larger \(c_\epsilon\) makes IPW-O-W soften earlier after its \(\Gamma=2\!-\!3\) peak and pushes DR-O-W's level down. The headline \(c_\epsilon=1\) is the tight operating point where both O-W methods clearly beat all others.
  • A capacity cap pins the treat-fraction and dampens the post-peak softening; it is cleanest uncapped. Both regimes are reported as-is.
