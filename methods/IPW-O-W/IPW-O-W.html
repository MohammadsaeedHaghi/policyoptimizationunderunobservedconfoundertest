R-OW

Robust, Odds-box ∩ Wasserstein value maximiser — the project's RW method (formerly srpo.multiarm.solve_rw_multiarm) in the code-1.1 clean form. This page states exactly the in-sample optimisation problem the two implementations in this folder solve: Capped/r_ow_capped.py and Uncapped/r_ow_uncapped.py.

One sentence. R-OW learns the treatment policy that maximises the worst-case inverse-propensity-weighted value on the training sample, where the worst case ranges over a set of re-weightings that captures (i) unobserved confounding (a Rosenbaum/Tan sensitivity box of size \(\Gamma\)) and (ii) covariate imbalance (a per-arm Wasserstein ball of radius \(\varepsilon_k\)) — optionally subject to per-arm capacity.

The method does no statistical preprocessing; it consumes quantities prepared by common/:

2 · The decision: a policy on the (discretised) support

We learn \(\pi_k(x)\in[0,1]\), the probability of assigning arm \(k\) at covariate \(x\), with \(\sum_{k}\pi_k(x)=1\). The discretize/mesh arguments fix where \(\pi\) is defined: with discretize=True the covariates are snapped onto a mesh-level grid (common.support), and units that share a grid cell are tied to one policy value — so \(\pi\) is a genuine piecewise-constant function of \(x\). With discretize=False the raw covariates are used (every unit its own cell), which on continuous \(x\) is the degenerate "singleton" regime.

3 · The in-sample problem (primal: worst-case value)

For a re-weighting \(W=(W_1,\dots,W_n)\) of the sample, the IPW value of \(\pi\) is

\[ V(\pi,W)\;=\;\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

The nominal weighting is \(W=\hat w\). Because of the unobserved confounder the true weighting is unknown; R-OW only assumes it lies in the uncertainty set \(\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\) and optimises the worst case over it:

\[ \boxed{\;\pi^\star \;=\; \arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\;} \]

where the feasible policy set \(\Pi\) is

\[ \Pi:\quad \pi_k(x_i)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\pi_k(x_i)=1\ \ \forall i,\qquad \underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_k(x_i)\;\le\;\mathrm{cap}_k\ \ \forall k}_{\textbf{capped version only}} , \]

plus the tie constraints (\(\pi\) constant on each grid cell). The uncertainty set combines two pieces:

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\;=\;\underbrace{\Big\{\,W:\ a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i\,\Big\}}_{\textbf{(O) odds / MSM box — unobserved confounding}}\ \cap\ \underbrace{\Big\{\,W:\ \mathcal W_{D}\big(\text{emp. law},\,W\text{-reweighted arm-}k\text{ law}\big)\le \varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k\,\Big\}}_{\textbf{(W) Wasserstein balls — covariate balance}} . \]

The (O) box is the Rosenbaum/Tan marginal-sensitivity model: under an unobserved confounder shifting the treatment odds by at most \(\Gamma\), the true inverse weight of each unit stays in \([a_i,b_i]\). The (W) balls additionally forbid the adversary from re-weighting in a way that distorts the within-arm covariate distribution by more than \(\varepsilon_k\) in Wasserstein distance (using the ground cost \(D\)) — this is the covariate-balance term, the "W" that distinguishes R-OW from R-O.

4 · What is actually solved (dual LP)

Dualising the inner \(\min_W\) turns the max–min into a single linear program — the LP these files build and hand to Gurobi. Variables: the policy \(\pi_k(x_i)\); \(\beta_k\ge0\); \(\gamma_{k,i}\); \(\theta_{k,j}\) (for factual \(j\in I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\)); \(\mu_i\ge0\); \(\nu_i\ge0\).

\[ \max_{\pi,\beta,\gamma,\theta,\mu,\nu}\ \ \sum_{k}\Big(-\beta_k\,\varepsilon_k+\frac1n\sum_i \gamma_{k,i}\Big)\;+\;\sum_i\big(\mu_i\,a_i-\nu_i\,b_i\big) \]

subject to

\[ \begin{aligned} &\textstyle\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)=1,\quad \pi_k(x_i)\in[0,1],\quad \pi \text{ tied on each cell}; &&\text{(valid policy)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\frac1n\sum_i \pi_k(x_i)\le \mathrm{cap}_k\quad\forall k; &&\textbf{(capacity — capped only)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\frac1n\,\pi_k(x_i)\,Y_i+\frac1n\,\theta_{k,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\ \ge\ 0\quad \forall k,\ \forall i\in I_k; &&\text{(dual feasibility, factual rows)}\\[2pt] &\beta_k\,D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0\quad \forall k,\ \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k; &&\text{(transport / Wasserstein geometry)}\\[2pt] &\beta_k\ge 0,\quad \mu_i\ge 0,\quad \nu_i\ge 0. \end{aligned} \]
dual variablemeaning (saved as the dual certificate per the saving spec)
\(\beta_k\ge0\)price of the Wasserstein radius \(\varepsilon_k\) (the "W" multiplier)
\(\mu_i,\ \nu_i\ge0\)multipliers on the box lower/upper bounds \(a_i,b_i\) (the "O" box)
\(\gamma_{k,i},\ \theta_{k,j}\)transport "demand"/"factual" duals certifying the optimal worst-case coupling

This LP is reproduced exactly from srpo.multiarm.solve_rw_multiarm — verified numerically: on identical inputs the new and old policies/objective/duals agree to machine precision (\(\max|\Delta\pi|=0\)).

5 · How it is solved — dualising the box ∩ Wasserstein adversary

R-OW is solved as one linear program, exactly like R-O, but the inner adversary now also moves a transport plan: it may re-weight the sample (the box) and re-couple it across covariates (the Wasserstein balls). The five steps below show how the max–min of section 3 collapses into the dual LP of section 4.

Step 1 — the primal max–min. Start from the worst-case value of section 3, written with the adversary moving the re-weighting \(w\) inside the intersection of the box set and the Wasserstein set:

\[ \max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{w\,\in\,\mathcal U_{\mathrm{box}}\,\cap\,\mathcal U_{\mathrm W}}\ V(\pi,w), \qquad V(\pi,w)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,w_i\,Y_i . \]

Step 2 — the Wasserstein set as a transport program. For each arm \(k\) the balance ball is expressed through a coupling \(\zeta^{k}_{ij}\ge0\), where \(i\) runs over all units and \(j\) over the arm-\(k\) factual units \(I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\). It must match a uniform demand marginal, the \(w\)-induced supply marginal, and stay within the transport budget \(\varepsilon_k\):

\[ \begin{aligned} &\zeta^{k}_{ij}\ge 0\quad \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k; &&\text{(coupling)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_{j\in I_k}\zeta^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac1n\quad \forall i; &&\text{(demand marginals)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_{i}\zeta^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac{w_j}{n}\quad \forall j\in I_k; &&\text{(supply marginals — couple to }w_j)\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_{i,j} D_{ij}\,\zeta^{k}_{ij}\ \le\ \varepsilon_k . &&\text{(transport budget)} \end{aligned} \]

So the inner problem is a joint minimisation over both the re-weighting \(w\) and the transport plans \(\zeta\): \(\ \min_{w,\zeta}\,V(\pi,w)\) subject to the box on \(w\) and the constraints above.

Step 3 — dualise the inner LP. Attach multipliers to each inner constraint: \(\beta_k\ge0\) on the budget \(\varepsilon_k\); \(\gamma_{k,i}\) (free) on the demand marginals; \(\theta_{k,j}\) (free) on the supply marginals — these are the ones that couple back to \(w_j\); and \(\mu_i\ge0,\ \nu_i\ge0\) on the box bounds \(a_i,b_i\). Eliminating the coupling \(\zeta\) leaves the transport-metric inequalities, and eliminating \(w\) against its box leaves the factual-row dual-feasibility conditions:

\[ \begin{aligned} &\beta_k\,D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0\quad \forall k,\ \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k; &&\text{(eliminate }\zeta\text{: transport metric)}\\[2pt] &\tfrac1n\,\pi_{k,i}\,Y_i+\tfrac1n\,\theta_{k,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\ \ge\ 0\quad \forall i\in I_k. &&\text{(eliminate }w\text{: factual rows)} \end{aligned} \]

Step 4 — the dual objective. The value of the dualised inner problem, to be maximised jointly with the outer \(\max_\pi\) (subject to the simplex, tie and capacity constraints of \(\Pi\)), is

\[ -\sum_{k}\beta_k\,\varepsilon_k\;+\;\frac1n\sum_{k,i}\gamma_{k,i}\;+\;\sum_i\big(a_i\,\mu_i-b_i\,\nu_i\big). \]

This is exactly the single LP written in section 4 — the outer policy variables and the inner dual variables are optimised together in one program.

Step 5 — solve. The LP is handed to Gurobi with DualReductions=0 (so the solver reports a clean primal/dual status rather than the ambiguous "infeasible-or-unbounded"). The radius \(\varepsilon_k\) is the tight per-arm radius returned by common.tight_epsilon (scaled by c_eps).

How the solution is used. The solve returns the policy \(\pi\) (a \(K\times n\) array) together with the full dual certificate \((\beta,\gamma,\theta,\mu,\nu)\). Off-support deployment to new covariates is then carried out separately via Shapley/KNN extension (extensions/).

6 · Capped vs. Uncapped — the only difference

The two files are identical except for the capacity row above:

  • Capped/r_ow_capped.py — adds \(\frac1n\sum_i\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\). The policy may treat at most a \(\mathrm{cap}_k\) fraction with arm \(k\) (control is typically left uncapped). Requires the cap argument.
  • Uncapped/r_ow_uncapped.py — drops that constraint entirely (no cap argument). The policy may assign any arm to any fraction of the population.

7 · Arguments that shape the problem

argumenteffect on the in-sample problem
discretize (bool)Discretisation switch. True: snap \(x\) to a grid (via support.py) and tie per cell — \(\pi\) is piecewise-constant and the LP is well-posed. False: raw \(x\), singleton support (the degenerate regime).
mesh (int)grid resolution: number of levels per coordinate when discretize=True (e.g. 6 ⇒ a 6×6 grid in 2-D). Finer mesh ⇒ more cells, fewer units per cell.
Gammasize of the (O) box. \(\Gamma=1\) collapses the box to a point (\(a_i=b_i=\hat w_i\)); R-OW then reduces to the non-robust IPW value maximiser.
c_eps / epsilonscale of / override for the (W) radius \(\varepsilon_k\). Larger \(\varepsilon_k\) ⇒ a weaker balance constraint.
zscore / metricGround-cost geometry (the distance matrix \(D\) the (W) term is built on, via common.geometry). zscore=True standardises the covariates first, so a large-unit feature cannot dominate the metric; zscore=False (the solver default) uses raw-\(x\) Euclidean distances — bit-identical to the original solver. metric selects the distance (only "euclidean" implemented). Supplied by GeometryConfig, whose default is zscore=True.

Outputs: the optimal on-support policy \(\pi^\star\) (K×n), its realised per-arm usage, the worst-case objective, and the full dual certificate \((\beta,\mu,\nu,\gamma,\theta)\) — all persisted per results/SAVING.md. Off-support deployment to new covariates is a separate step (extensions/, Shapley/KNN), not part of this method.
