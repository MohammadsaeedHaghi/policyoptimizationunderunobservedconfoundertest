Hajek-O-X

Robust, Odds-box regret minimiser — the Kallus & Zhou regret-minimiser over the odds box only (formerly srpo.multiarm.solve_kallus_multiarm). This page states exactly the in-sample optimisation problem the two files in this folder solve: Capped/regret_o_capped.py and Uncapped/regret_o_uncapped.py.

One sentence. Hajek-O-X learns the policy that minimises the worst-case regret \(R(\pi,W)=V(\pi_0,W)-V(\pi,W)\) versus the all-control baseline \(\pi_0\), where the worst case ranges over a Rosenbaum/Tan odds box of size \(\Gamma\) (unobserved confounding) together with a per-arm Hájek calibration — but, like R-O and unlike R-OW, with no Wasserstein covariate-balance ball. Optionally subject to per-arm capacity.

1 · Inputs (all fixed before the optimiser runs)

The method does no statistical preprocessing; it consumes quantities prepared by common/:

Note what is absent versus R-OW: there is no distance matrix \(D\) and no Wasserstein radius \(\varepsilon_k\) — Hajek-O-X drops the covariate-balance term entirely, so it has no c_eps/epsilon argument.

2 · The decision: a policy on the (discretised) support

We learn \(\pi_k(x)\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_k\pi_k(x)=1\). The discretize/mesh arguments fix where \(\pi\) is defined exactly as in R-O/R-OW: discretize=True snaps the covariates onto a mesh-level grid (common.support) and ties same-cell units to one policy value (so \(\pi\) is piecewise-constant in \(x\)); discretize=False uses the raw covariates (singleton support — degenerate on continuous \(x\)). The discretisation is still needed even though Hajek-O-X has no Wasserstein term: it is what pools units so the free-π LP is well-posed.

3 · The in-sample problem (primal: worst-case SELF-NORMALISED regret)

This is the Kallus & Zhou Hájek (self-normalised) regret estimator: the per-treatment value is normalised by the same weights' own mass, so the denominator floats with \(W\). Per arm \(t\),

\[ \hat R^{(t)}_{\pi_0}(\pi;W)=\frac{\sum_{i:T_i=t}\big(\pi_0(t\mid x_i)-\pi(t\mid x_i)\big)\,Y_i\,W_i}{\sum_{i:T_i=t} W_i}, \qquad \pi_0=\text{all-control}\ (\pi_{0,0}\equiv1), \]

and the total worst-case regret is \(\bar R(\pi;W)=\sum_t \sup_{W\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)} \hat R^{(t)}_{\pi_0}(\pi;W)\) (the sup separates over arms — each arm's units carry disjoint weights). Hajek-O-X minimises it:

\[ \boxed{\;\pi^\star \;=\; \arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \sum_t\ \sup_{W\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)}\ \frac{\sum_{i:T_i=t}\big(\mathbf 1[t=0]-\pi_{t}(x_i)\big)Y_iW_i}{\sum_{i:T_i=t}W_i}\;} \]

with the feasible policy set

\[ \Pi:\quad \pi_k(x_i)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{k}\pi_k(x_i)=1\ \ \forall i,\qquad \underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_{i}\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\ \ \forall k}_{\textbf{capped version only}}, \]

(plus the tie constraints), and the box-only uncertainty set (no calibration constraint — the self-normalisation IS the calibration; the box is built on the raw inverse weights \(\tilde W_i=1/\hat P(T_i\mid x_i)\ge1\)):

\[ \mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)\;=\;\big\{\,W:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\textbf{(O) odds / MSM box, } a_i\ge1>0}\,\big\}. \]

Unlike the value method R-O (which fixes the denominator with a hard \(\sum_{T_i=k}W_i=n\) calibration), the Hájek regret lets the denominator float and optimises the ratio (Kallus & Zhou Eq. 4, 10). Because \(\pi_0\) is itself feasible, the optimal worst-case regret is \(\le 0\) (do-no-harm). NB: here \(Y\) is a reward (high = good), the opposite of the paper's loss — hence \((\mathbf 1[t=0]-\pi_t)\), not \((\pi_t-\mathbf 1[t=0])\).

4 · What is actually solved (Charnes–Cooper dual LP)

Each per-treatment \(\sup_W\) is a linear-fractional program; the Charnes–Cooper transform linearises it and its dual (Kallus & Zhou Eq. 12) introduces, per arm \(t\), a level \(\lambda_t=\hat Q_t\) (the worst-case regret of that arm) and box duals \(u_i,v_i\ge0\). The outer \(\min_\pi\) fuses with these per-arm minimisations into one LP:

\[ \min_{\pi,\,u,v\ge0,\,\lambda}\ \ \sum_{t=0}^{K-1}\lambda_t \]

subject to

\[ \begin{aligned} &v_i-u_i+\lambda_{T_i}\ \ge\ \big(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\big)\,Y_i\quad\forall i; &&\text{(CC coupling, per unit)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_{i\in I_t}\big(u_i\,a_i - v_i\,b_i\big)\ \ge\ 0\quad\forall t; &&\text{(CC scaling / box-product)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)=1,\quad \pi_k(x_i)\in[0,1],\quad \pi \text{ tied on each cell}; &&\text{(valid policy)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\frac1n\sum_i \pi_k(x_i)\le \mathrm{cap}_k\quad\forall k; &&\textbf{(capacity — capped only)}\\[2pt] &u_i\ge 0,\quad v_i\ge 0,\quad \lambda_t\ \text{free}. \end{aligned} \]
dual variablemeaning (saved as the dual certificate per the saving spec)
\(u_i,\ v_i\ge0\)Charnes–Cooper duals on the lower/upper bounds \(a_i,b_i\) of the "O" box — stored in the mu/nu result fields
\(\lambda_t\) (free sign)per-treatment worst-case regret level \(\hat Q_t\) (the self-normalised \(\sup_W\)); stored in beta

The RHS \(\big(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i}\big)Y_i\) is the per-unit regret reward (control baseline minus policy), in the reward convention (high \(Y\) = good). With \(a_i\ge1\) (box on raw weights) the floating denominator stays positive, so the ratio is bounded. At \(\Gamma=1\) the box is a point and Hajek-O-X reduces to the IPW maximiser.

5 · How it is solved — dualising the inner regret-adversary

Hajek-O-X is a MIN problem (minimise the worst-case regret) yet it is solved as a single LP. The trick is to dualise the inner adversary so its maximisation turns into a minimisation that fuses with the outer min over \(\pi\). The five steps below trace that collapse; the result is exactly the dual LP of the previous section.

Step 1 — the primal (a min–max). Write the worst-case regret directly as a saddle problem:

\[ \min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \max_{W\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)}\ R(\pi,W),\qquad R(\pi,W)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\big)\,Y_i\,W_i, \]

where \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)\) is the same box+calibration set as R-O (\(a_i\le W_i\le b_i\), \(\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\)).

Step 2 — the inner problem is an LP (a MAX over \(W\)). Freeze \(\pi\). The bracket multiplying \(W_i\) is then a fixed coefficient \(d_i\), so the adversary solves a linear program in \(W\):

\[ \max_{W}\ d^\top W\quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i,\quad \sum_{i\in I_k} W_i=n\ \ \forall k, \qquad d_i=\frac1n\big(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\big)\,Y_i, \]

where \(I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\) indexes the units on arm \(k\).

Step 3 — Charnes–Cooper linearise the ratio, then dualise. With \(r_i=(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i})Y_i\) the inner per-arm problem is \(\hat Q_t=\sup_{a\le W\le b}\ \sum_{I_t}r_iW_i/\sum_{I_t}W_i\). Substituting \(w=W/\sum W\) (so \(\sum_{I_t}w=1\), \(sa_i\le w_i\le sb_i\) with a free scale \(s\ge0\)) makes it the LP \(\max_{w,s}\sum r_iw_i\); its dual (Kallus & Zhou Eq. 12) is

\[ \hat Q_t\;=\;\min_{u,v\ge0,\ \lambda_t}\ \lambda_t\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad v_i-u_i+\lambda_t\ge r_i\ \ \forall i\in I_t,\qquad \textstyle\sum_{i\in I_t}\big(u_i a_i-v_i b_i\big)\ge0. \]

Step 4 — merge with the outer \(\min_\pi\). Both the outer policy choice and each dualised inner problem are minimisations, so they combine. Since \(r_i\) is linear in \(\pi\), the constraint \(v_i-u_i+\lambda_{T_i}\ge(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i})Y_i\) is linear, and the whole saddle problem becomes the single MIN over \((\pi,u,v,\lambda)\) of Section 4. Its optimum \(\sum_t\hat Q_t\) is the worst-case self-normalised regret, \(\le 0\) because \(\pi_0\) is always feasible (do-no-harm).

Step 5 — solve. The merged LP is handed to Gurobi (with DualReductions=0). On return the box duals \(u,v\) are stored in mu/nu and the per-treatment regret levels \(\lambda_t\) in beta.

How it is used. The solver returns the on-support policy \(\pi\) (K×n) together with the duals \((\lambda,u,v)\); off-support deployment to new covariates is a separate step via the Shapley/KNN extensions.

6 · Capped vs. Uncapped — the only difference

  • Capped/regret_o_capped.py — adds \(\frac1n\sum_i\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\) (requires cap).
  • Uncapped/regret_o_uncapped.py — drops it (no cap argument); any arm may be assigned to any fraction.

7 · Hajek-O-X vs IPW-O-X vs IPW-O-W

Same box-only uncertainty set, same discretisation and capacity as R-O — the difference is the objective and the normalisation. R-O maximises worst-case value with a hard \(\sum_{T_i=k}W_i=n\) calibration (a MAX LP); Hajek-O-X minimises worst-case self-normalised regret against the all-control baseline — the Hájek ratio whose denominator floats (a MIN LP, do-no-harm \(\le0\)). Like R-O it omits the Wasserstein covariate-balance ball of R-OW: no \(D\), no \(\varepsilon_k\), no transport duals; the per-treatment levels \(\lambda_t\) and box duals \(u,v\) come from the Charnes–Cooper dual above. At \(\Gamma=1\) the box collapses and Hajek-O-X reduces to the non-robust IPW value maximiser.
