R-O

Robust, Odds-box value maximiser — R-OW without the Wasserstein covariate-balance term (formerly srpo.multiarm.solve_rw_no_wasserstein_multiarm). This page states exactly the in-sample optimisation problem the two files in this folder solve: Capped/r_o_capped.py and Uncapped/r_o_uncapped.py.

One sentence. R-O learns the policy that maximises the worst-case inverse-propensity-weighted value on the training sample, where the worst case ranges over a Rosenbaum/Tan odds box of size \(\Gamma\) (unobserved confounding) together with a per-arm Hájek calibration — but, unlike R-OW, with no Wasserstein covariate-balance ball. Optionally subject to per-arm capacity.

1 · Inputs (all fixed before the optimiser runs)

The method does no statistical preprocessing; it consumes quantities prepared by common/:

\(x_i,\;T_i,\;Y_i\)covariates, observed arm, observed outcome of unit \(i=1,\dots,n\); arms \(k=0,\dots,K-1\)data
\(\hat w_i = 1/\hat P(T_i\mid x_i)\)nominal inverse weight, per-arm Hájek-normalised (\(\sum_{i:T_i=k}\hat w_i=n\)). Estimated from \((X,T)\) — never the true propensity.common.ipw_weights_from_data

Note what is absent versus R-OW: there is no distance matrix \(D\) and no Wasserstein radius \(\varepsilon_k\) — R-O drops the covariate-balance term entirely, so it has no c_eps/epsilon argument.

2 · The decision: a policy on the (discretised) support

We learn \(\pi_k(x)\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_k\pi_k(x)=1\). The discretize/mesh arguments fix where \(\pi\) is defined exactly as in R-OW: discretize=True snaps the covariates onto a mesh-level grid (common.support) and ties same-cell units to one policy value (so \(\pi\) is piecewise-constant in \(x\)); discretize=False uses the raw covariates (singleton support — degenerate on continuous \(x\)). The discretisation is still needed even though R-O has no Wasserstein term: it is what pools units so the free-π LP is well-posed.

3 · The in-sample problem (primal: worst-case value)

For a re-weighting \(W\) of the sample the IPW value of \(\pi\) is \(V(\pi,W)=\frac1n\sum_i W_i Y_i \pi_{T_i}(x_i)\). R-O maximises the worst case over the box-only uncertainty set:

\[ \boxed{\;\pi^\star \;=\; \arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)}\ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\;} \]

with the feasible policy set

\[ \Pi:\quad \pi_k(x_i)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{k}\pi_k(x_i)=1\ \ \forall i,\qquad \underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_{i}\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\ \ \forall k}_{\textbf{capped version only}}, \]

(plus the tie constraints), and the box-only uncertainty set

\[ \mathcal U_{\mathrm O}(\Gamma)\;=\;\Big\{\,W:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\textbf{(O) odds / MSM box}},\quad \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k} W_i = n\ \ \forall k}_{\textbf{per-arm Hájek calibration}}\,\Big\}. \]

This is exactly R-OW's set with the Wasserstein balls dropped (equivalently \(\varepsilon_k\to\infty\), so the balance constraint never binds). The (O) box is the Rosenbaum/Tan marginal-sensitivity model; the calibration keeps each arm's worst-case weights summing to \(n\).

4 · What is actually solved (dual LP)

Dualising the inner \(\min_W\) gives the LP the files build. Variables: \(\pi_k(x_i)\); \(\mu_i\ge0\); \(\nu_i\ge0\); and a free-sign per-arm calibration multiplier \(\beta_k\).

\[ \max_{\pi,\mu,\nu,\beta}\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(a_i\,\mu_i-b_i\,\nu_i\big)\;+\;\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} n\,\beta_k \]

subject to

\[ \begin{aligned} &\mu_i-\nu_i \;=\; \tfrac1n\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\;-\;\beta_{T_i}\quad\forall i; &&\text{(stationarity, per unit)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)=1,\quad \pi_k(x_i)\in[0,1],\quad \pi \text{ tied on each cell}; &&\text{(valid policy)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\frac1n\sum_i \pi_k(x_i)\le \mathrm{cap}_k\quad\forall k; &&\textbf{(capacity — capped only)}\\[2pt] &\mu_i\ge 0,\quad \nu_i\ge 0,\quad \beta_k\ \text{free}. \end{aligned} \]
dual variablemeaning (saved as the dual certificate per the saving spec)
\(\mu_i,\ \nu_i\ge0\)multipliers on the box lower/upper bounds \(a_i,b_i\) (the "O" box)
\(\beta_k\) (free sign)per-arm Hájek-calibration multiplier — not a Wasserstein radius (contrast R-OW, where \(\beta_k\ge0\) prices \(\varepsilon_k\))

Reproduced exactly from srpo.multiarm.solve_rw_no_wasserstein_multiarm — verified numerically: new vs old agree to machine precision (\(\max|\Delta\pi|=\max|\Delta\beta|=\max|\Delta\mu|=0\)).

5 · How it is solved — dualising the inner adversary

R-O is solved as one linear program, obtained by dualising the inner \(\min_W\) and merging it with the outer \(\max_\pi\). The steps below trace how the primal of section 3 becomes the dual LP of section 4 (we write \(w\) for the adversary's weight vector).

Step 1 — primal (max–min). Maximise the worst-case IPW value over the box-only set:
\[ \max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{w\in\mathcal U}\ V(\pi,w),\qquad V(\pi,w)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,w_i\,Y_i, \]
\[ \mathcal U=\Big\{\,w:\ a_i\le w_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i,\quad \textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k} w_i = n\ \ \forall k\,\Big\}. \]
Step 2 — the inner problem is an LP in \(w\). For a fixed \(\pi\) the inner minimisation is a linear program in \(w\) with cost \(c^\top w\), where
\[ c_i=\tfrac1n\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,Y_i, \]
subject to the box \(a\le w\le b\) and the per-arm equalities \(\sum_{i:T_i=k} w_i=n\).
Step 3 — dualise the inner LP. Attach multipliers \(\mu_i\ge0\) to \(w_i\ge a_i\), \(\nu_i\ge0\) to \(w_i\le b_i\), and a free \(\beta_k\) to the arm-\(k\) equality. LP strong duality turns the inner min into a max:
\[ \min_{w\in\mathcal U} c^\top w \;=\; \max_{\mu,\nu\ge0,\ \beta}\ \sum_{i}\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+\sum_{k} n\,\beta_k \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mu_i-\nu_i+\beta_{T_i}=c_i\ \ \forall i. \]
Step 4 — merge with the outer \(\max_\pi\). Substituting \(c_i=\tfrac1n\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,Y_i\) and rearranging the equality gives the per-unit stationarity constraint
\[ \mu_i-\nu_i=\tfrac1n\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\beta_{T_i}, \]
so the whole max–min collapses into a single maximisation over \((\pi,\mu,\nu,\beta)\) — exactly the LP of section 4 — with the policy constraints (simplex, per-cell tie, capacity) added.
Step 5 — solve. The merged LP is handed to Gurobi. We set DualReductions=0 so the dual certificate \((\beta,\mu,\nu)\) is well defined.

How it is used. The solve returns the on-support policy \(\pi\) (\(K\times n\)) together with the duals; deployment to new covariates is done with the Shapley/KNN extension.

6 · Capped vs. Uncapped — the only difference

  • Capped/r_o_capped.py — adds \(\frac1n\sum_i\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\) (requires cap).
  • Uncapped/r_o_uncapped.py — drops it (no cap argument); any arm may be assigned to any fraction.

7 · R-O vs R-OW

Same robust value-maximisation, same box, same discretisation and capacity — R-O simply omits the Wasserstein covariate-balance ball. Consequences: no \(D\), no \(\varepsilon_k\), no \(\beta_k\ge0\) radius dual and no transport (\(\gamma,\theta\)) duals; instead \(\beta_k\) becomes the free-sign calibration multiplier and the coupling is the single per-unit stationarity equation above. R-OW \(\ge\) R-O in robustness modelling (it additionally controls covariate imbalance); comparing the two isolates the value of the Wasserstein term. At \(\Gamma=1\) both collapse to the non-robust IPW value maximiser.
