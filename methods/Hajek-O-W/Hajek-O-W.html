Hajek-OW

Robust, Odds-box ∩ Wasserstein worst-case-regret minimiser — the Kallus & Zhou regret formulation (formerly srpo.multiarm.solve_kallus_w_multiarm) in the code-1.1 clean form. It uses the same uncertainty set as R-OW, but minimises worst-case regret against the all-control baseline rather than maximising worst-case value. This page states exactly the in-sample optimisation problem the two implementations in this folder solve: Capped/regret_ow_capped.py and Uncapped/regret_ow_uncapped.py.

One sentence. Hajek-OW learns the treatment policy that minimises the worst-case regret — the gap to the all-control baseline \(\pi_0\) — of its inverse-propensity-weighted value on the training sample, where the worst case ranges over the same re-weighting set as R-OW: (i) a Rosenbaum/Tan sensitivity box of size \(\Gamma\) (unobserved confounding) intersected with (ii) per-arm Wasserstein balls of radius \(\varepsilon_k\) (covariate imbalance) — optionally subject to per-arm capacity.
An intentional hybrid — not pure Kallus. Hajek-OW deliberately combines two ingredients:
  • Kallus & Zhou's objective — worst-case regret against the all-control baseline \(\pi_0\) (the paper's exact criterion), so the optimum is always do-no-harm (objective \(\le 0\)).
  • R-OW's uncertainty set (the "OW") — the Tan/Rosenbaum odds box of size \(\Gamma\) intersected with per-arm Wasserstein balls of radius \(\varepsilon_k\), rather than Kallus's own extension (a total-variation budget on the weights).
Crucially the policy class is left unrestricted (free \(\pi\) per support cell, solved by LP) — not the parametric softmax of the Kallus method — precisely so that a per-arm capacity constraint can be added (the Capped/ solver). So: Kallus-style regret objective, R-OW-style confounding+balance uncertainty, capacity-capable free policy. Pure parametric Kallus lives in methods/Kallus/; the box-only regret minimiser is Hajek-O-X.

2 · The decision: a policy on the (discretised) support

We learn \(\pi_k(x)\in[0,1]\), the probability of assigning arm \(k\) at covariate \(x\), with \(\sum_{k}\pi_k(x)=1\). The discretize/mesh arguments fix where \(\pi\) is defined: with discretize=True the covariates are snapped onto a mesh-level grid (common.support), and units that share a grid cell are tied to one policy value — so \(\pi\) is a genuine piecewise-constant function of \(x\). With discretize=False the raw covariates are used (every unit its own cell), which on continuous \(x\) is the degenerate "singleton" regime.

3 · The in-sample problem (primal: worst-case regret)

For a re-weighting \(W=(W_1,\dots,W_n)\) of the sample, the IPW value of \(\pi\) is

\[ V(\pi,W)\;=\;\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n} W_i\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

The regret of \(\pi\) under weighting \(W\) is its shortfall against the all-control baseline \(\pi_0\) (which assigns arm 0 to everyone, so \(V(\pi_0,W)=\frac1n\sum_{i:T_i=0}W_iY_i\)):

\[ R(\pi,W)\;=\;V(\pi_0,W)\;-\;V(\pi,W). \]

Because of the unobserved confounder the true weighting \(W\) is unknown; Hajek-OW only assumes it lies in the uncertainty set \(\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\) and minimises the worst-case regret over it:

\[ \boxed{\;\pi^\star \;=\; \arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \max_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\ \Big[\,V(\pi_0,W)-V(\pi,W)\,\Big]\;} \]

where the feasible policy set \(\Pi\) is

\[ \Pi:\quad \pi_k(x_i)\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\pi_k(x_i)=1\ \ \forall i,\qquad \underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_k(x_i)\;\le\;\mathrm{cap}_k\ \ \forall k}_{\textbf{capped version only}} , \]

plus the tie constraints (\(\pi\) constant on each grid cell). The uncertainty set is identical to R-OW's and combines two pieces:

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\;=\;\underbrace{\Big\{\,W:\ a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i\,\Big\}}_{\textbf{(O) odds / MSM box — unobserved confounding}}\ \cap\ \underbrace{\Big\{\,W:\ \mathcal W_{D}\big(\text{emp. law},\,W\text{-reweighted arm-}k\text{ law}\big)\le \varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k\,\Big\}}_{\textbf{(W) Wasserstein balls — covariate balance}} . \]

Already self-normalised (Hájek). The Wasserstein transport's mass-balance (demand \(\tfrac1n\), supply \(W_j/n\)) forces \(\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\) per arm — so the \(\frac1n\)-normalised value \(V(\pi,W)\) equals the per-treatment Hájek ratio \(\sum_{T_i=k}r_iW_i/\sum_{T_i=k}W_i\) of Kallus & Zhou (Eq. 4, 10). The calibration is supplied by the transport, so — unlike box-only Hajek-O-X — no separate handling is needed and the original dual LP below is unchanged. (\(Y\) is a reward here, hence \(\mathbf 1[k{=}0]-\pi_k\).)

Do-no-harm. Since the all-control baseline \(\pi_0\) is itself a feasible policy (\(\pi_0\in\Pi\)), choosing \(\pi=\pi_0\) gives \(R(\pi_0,W)=0\) for every \(W\); the optimal worst-case regret is therefore always \(\le 0\). As \(\Gamma\) grows and the adversary gets stronger, the optimum reverts toward \(\pi_0\) — Hajek-OW will not deploy a treatment unless it can certify a robust improvement over control.

4 · What is actually solved (MIN dual LP)

Dualising the inner \(\max_W\) of the regret turns the min–max into a single minimisation linear program — the LP these files build and hand to Gurobi. The inner reward of unit \(i\) under arm \(k\) is \(c_{k,i}=\frac1n\big(\mathbf 1[k=0]-\pi_k(x_i)\big)Y_i\). Variables: the policy \(\pi_k(x_i)\); \(\beta_k\ge0\); \(\gamma_{k,i}\); \(\theta_{k,j}\) (for factual \(j\in I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\)); \(p_i\ge0\); \(q_i\ge0\).

\[ \min_{\pi,\beta,\gamma,\theta,p,q}\ \ \sum_{k}\Big(\beta_k\,\varepsilon_k+\frac1n\sum_i \gamma_{k,i}\Big)\;+\;\sum_i\big(b_i\,p_i-a_i\,q_i\big) \]

subject to

\[ \begin{aligned} &\textstyle\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)=1,\quad \pi_k(x_i)\in[0,1],\quad \pi \text{ tied on each cell}; &&\text{(valid policy)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle\frac1n\sum_i \pi_k(x_i)\le \mathrm{cap}_k\quad\forall k; &&\textbf{(capacity — capped only)}\\[2pt] &\textstyle p_i-q_i-\frac1n\,\theta_{k,i}+\frac1n\,\pi_k(x_i)\,Y_i\ =\ \frac1n\,\mathbf 1[k=0]\,Y_i\quad \forall k,\ \forall i\in I_k; &&\text{(factual-row dual equality)}\\[2pt] &\beta_k\,D_{ij}+\gamma_{k,i}+\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0\quad \forall k,\ \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k; &&\text{(transport / Wasserstein geometry)}\\[2pt] &\beta_k\ge 0,\quad p_i\ge 0,\quad q_i\ge 0. \end{aligned} \]
dual variablemeaning (saved as the dual certificate per the saving spec)
\(\beta_k\ge0\)price of the Wasserstein radius \(\varepsilon_k\) (the "W" multiplier)
\(p_i,\ q_i\ge0\)Kallus regret-box duals on the box upper/lower bounds \(b_i,a_i\) — saved in the result fields mu (\(=p\)) and nu (\(=q\))
\(\gamma_{k,i},\ \theta_{k,j}\)transport "demand"/"factual" duals certifying the optimal worst-case coupling

This is a MIN problem (the objective is the worst-case regret, not value), which is the structural difference from R-OW's MAX LP. The objective value is \(\le 0\) at the optimum (do-no-harm: \(\pi_0\) is feasible). The LP is reproduced exactly from srpo.multiarm.solve_kallus_w_multiarm.

5 · How it is solved — dualising the box ∩ Wasserstein regret-adversary

Hajek-OW is R-O with the inner adversary additionally constrained to the per-arm Wasserstein ball: the same min–max regret, but the worst-case re-weighting must now respect both the odds box and covariate balance. Dualising that inner maximisation produces the single minimisation LP of the previous section. The derivation proceeds in five steps.

Step 1 — primal (min–max regret). Start from the worst-case regret over the box ∩ Wasserstein set,

\[ \min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \max_{w\,\in\,\mathcal U_{\mathrm{box}}\cap\,\mathcal U_{\mathrm W}}\ R(\pi,w), \qquad R(\pi,w)\;=\;\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathbf 1[T_i=0]-\pi_{T_i}\big)\,Y_i\,w_i, \]

with the per-arm Wasserstein set \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm W}\) exactly as in R-OW. This is a MIN LP: the outer player chooses the policy \(\pi\), the inner adversary chooses the re-weighting \(w\).

Step 2 — inner max over \((w,\zeta)\). The inner maximisation is lifted to the transport plane: for each arm \(k\) introduce the coupling \(\zeta^k\) whose marginals are the demand (target empirical law) and supply (the \(w\)-reweighted arm-\(k\) law), with transport budget \(\varepsilon_k\) — the same transport coupling \(\zeta^k\) with demand / supply marginals and budget \(\varepsilon_k\) as R-OW.

Step 3 — dualise. Attach multipliers to the inner program: \(\beta_k\ge 0\) to the transport budget, \(\gamma^{\mathrm d}_{k,i}\) to the demand marginals, \(\theta_{k,j}\) to the supply marginals, and \(p_i\ge 0,\ q_i\ge 0\) to the box upper/lower bounds. Strong duality (the inner program is a bounded LP) gives the factual-row equality, one per factual unit \(i\in I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\),

\[ p_i-q_i-\frac1n\,\theta_{k,i}+\frac1n\,Y_i\,\pi_{k,i}\ =\ \frac1n\,\mathbf 1[k=0]\,Y_i\qquad(i\in I_k), \]

together with the transport-metric inequality tying the budget price to the ground cost,

\[ \beta_k\,D_{ij}+\gamma^{\mathrm d}_{k,i}+\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0 . \]

Step 4 — objective (MIN). The dual objective collects the priced budget, the demand duals, and the box bounds,

\[ \sum_{k}\Big(\beta_k\,\varepsilon_k+\frac1n\sum_i \gamma^{\mathrm d}_{k,i}\Big)\;+\;\sum_i\big(b_i\,p_i-a_i\,q_i\big), \]

and is minimised jointly with the outer \(\min_\pi\) and the policy constraints (valid policy, ties, and capacity in the capped version). The result is precisely the LP written in the previous section.

Step 5 — solve. The LP is handed to Gurobi. At the optimum the objective equals the worst-case regret (\(\le 0\) by do-no-harm). The box duals \(p,q\) are stored as mu/nu; the transport duals \(\gamma^{\mathrm d},\theta\) are stored as gamma_dual/theta.

How it is used. The solve returns the on-support policy \(\pi\) (K×n) together with the dual certificate; deployment to off-support covariates is then carried out via the Shapley/KNN extension.

6 · Capped vs. Uncapped — the only difference

The two files are identical except for the capacity row above:

  • Capped/regret_ow_capped.py — adds \(\frac1n\sum_i\pi_k(x_i)\le\mathrm{cap}_k\). The policy may treat at most a \(\mathrm{cap}_k\) fraction with arm \(k\) (control is typically left uncapped). Requires the cap argument.
  • Uncapped/regret_ow_uncapped.py — drops that constraint entirely (no cap argument). The policy may assign any arm to any fraction of the population.

7 · Arguments that shape the problem

argumenteffect on the in-sample problem
discretize (bool)Discretisation switch. True: snap \(x\) to a grid (via support.py) and tie per cell — \(\pi\) is piecewise-constant and the LP is well-posed. False: raw \(x\), singleton support (the degenerate regime).
mesh (int)grid resolution: number of levels per coordinate when discretize=True (e.g. 6 ⇒ a 6×6 grid in 2-D). Finer mesh ⇒ more cells, fewer units per cell.
Gammasize of the (O) box. \(\Gamma=1\) collapses the box to a point (\(a_i=b_i=\hat w_i\)); the worst-case regret then has no confounding slack and the optimum tracks the non-robust regret-minimiser.
c_eps / epsilonscale of / override for the (W) radius \(\varepsilon_k\). Larger \(\varepsilon_k\) ⇒ a weaker balance constraint (a stronger adversary, costed in the objective by \(\beta_k\varepsilon_k\)).
zscore / metricGround-cost geometry (the distance matrix \(D\) the (W) term is built on, via common.geometry). zscore=True standardises the covariates first, so a large-unit feature cannot dominate the metric; zscore=False (the solver default) uses raw-\(x\) Euclidean distances — bit-identical to the original solver. metric selects the distance (only "euclidean" implemented). Supplied by GeometryConfig, whose default is zscore=True.
maximizeTrue (default): \(Y\) is a reward (regret \(V(\pi_0)-V(\pi)\), the studies' convention); False: \(Y\) is a loss — Kallus & Zhou's exact convention (regret \(V(\pi)-V(\pi_0)\)).

Outputs: the optimal on-support policy \(\pi^\star\) (K×n), its realised per-arm usage, the worst-case regret objective (\(\le 0\)), and the full dual certificate \((\beta,p,q,\gamma,\theta)\) (with \(p,q\) saved as mu,nu) — all persisted per results/SAVING.md. Off-support deployment to new covariates is a separate step (extensions/, Shapley/KNN), not part of this method.
