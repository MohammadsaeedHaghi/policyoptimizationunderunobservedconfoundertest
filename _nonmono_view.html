code 1.1 — Confounding-Robust Policy Optimization

Policy learning under unobserved confounding: robust (worst-case) value & regret methods over a sensitivity model intersected with a Wasserstein covariate-balance set, plus baselines and clairvoyant ceilings — and the shared pipeline that feeds them.

Every method consumes identical, pre-computed inputs from common/ and learns a policy \(\pi_k(x)\in[0,1]\), \(\sum_k\pi_k(x)=1\) (arm \(0\) = control). The confounder \(S\) is unobserved — propensities are always estimated from \((X,T)\). Start with Overview, then open any method's tab.

The pipeline

common/ turns raw data into the calibrated, robust-ready inputs every method shares; the solver returns an on-support policy + a dual certificate; an extension deploys it off-support; everything is persisted per results/SAVING.md.

The method family

The uncertainty set \(\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)\)

R-O / Regret-O use the box (O) alone; R-OW / Hajek-OW use the intersection (O)∩(W). At \(\Gamma=1\) the box collapses to the point \(\hat w\) and the robust methods reduce to IPW.

Shared building blocks

common/ — pre-algorithm

  • propensity: multinomial logistic \(\hat e(X)=P(T|X)\) — from \((X,T)\) only, never \(e_{\text{true}}\)/\(S\).
  • hajek: inverse weights \(\hat w\), per-arm \(\sum_{i:T_i=k}\hat w_i=n\).
  • geometry: \(D_{ij}=\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert\) (z-scoreable) — the Wasserstein ground cost.
  • support: snap continuous \(X\) to a grid + tie groups (the discretize switch).
  • sensitivity: matched \(\Gamma=e^{\gamma/2}\); MSM box \(a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1)\), \(b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1)\).
  • wasserstein_radius (tight \(\varepsilon_k\)) · outcome_model (\(\hat\mu\)) · diagnostics (overlap/ESS) · config/persistence.

extensions/ & conventions

  • Shapley: closed-form \(\pi^{S}(x)=\min_k\max_j A_{jk}(x)\). details →
  • KNN: inverse-distance \(k\)-NN of the support policy. details →
  • No true propensities — every weight is estimated from data.
  • maximize — \(Y\) as reward (default) vs the paper's loss convention.
  • Capped / Uncapped — per-arm budget \(\tfrac1n\sum_i\pi_k(x_i)\le\text{cap}_k\), or none.
  • obj_kind — each saved objective is tagged (not cross-comparable across kinds).
R-OW

Robust, odds-box ∩ Wasserstein — value maximiser

The project's “RW” method: maximise the worst-case inverse-propensity value over the sensitivity box intersected with per-arm Wasserstein covariate-balance balls.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i W_iY_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — the re-weightings \(W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}\) the adversary may pick:

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=\Big\{\,W:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \underbrace{\mathcal W_{D}\!\big(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_k,\,\mathbb{P}^{W}_k\big)\le\varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k}_{\text{(W) Wasserstein covariate balance}}\,\Big\} \]
\[ a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\qquad b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1),\qquad D_{ij}=\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert. \]

(O) is the Rosenbaum/Tan marginal-sensitivity box (unobserved confounding at level \(\Gamma\); \(\Gamma=1\Rightarrow a_i=b_i=\hat w_i\)). (W) bounds, per arm \(k\), the Wasserstein distance between the nominal arm-\(k\) covariate law \(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_k\) and its \(W\)-reweighting \(\mathbb{P}^{W}_k\) under ground cost \(D\). Feasible policies \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ per-arm capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the dual LP (Gurobi)

Dualising the inner \(\min_W\) (box + per-arm Wasserstein transport, by LP strong duality) collapses the max–min into a single MAX linear program. Variables: the policy \(\pi\); \(\beta_k\ge0\) (W-radius); \(\gamma_{k,i},\theta_{k,j}\) (transport, \(j\in I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\)); \(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0\) (box).

\[ \max_{\pi,\beta\ge0,\gamma,\theta,\mu\ge0,\nu\ge0}\ \sum_k\Big(-\beta_k\varepsilon_k+\tfrac1n\sum_i\gamma_{k,i}\Big)+\sum_i\big(\mu_i a_i-\nu_i b_i\big) \]

subject to a valid policy (\(\sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1,\ \pi_k\in[0,1]\), tied per cell; + capacity when Capped) and

\[ \begin{aligned} &\tfrac1n\pi_k(x_i)Y_i+\tfrac1n\theta_{k,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\ \ge\ 0 &&\forall k,\ \forall i\in I_k\quad\text{(factual rows)}\\[2pt] &\beta_k D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0 &&\forall k,\ \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k\quad\text{(transport / Wasserstein)} \end{aligned} \]

Gurobi (DualReductions=0). The dual certificate \((\beta,\mu,\nu,\gamma,\theta)\) is saved with \(\pi^\star\). Reproduces solve_rw_multiarm to machine precision.

R-O

Robust, odds-box only — value maximiser

R-OW with the Wasserstein term removed: the worst case ranges over the Marginal-Sensitivity box alone, plus the per-arm Hájek calibration \(\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\).

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma)}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i W_iY_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — the box only (no Wasserstein term):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma)=\Big\{\,W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}},\ \ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\ \ \forall k}_{\text{per-arm Hájek calibration}}\,\Big\} \]
\[ a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\qquad b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1). \]

Same Rosenbaum/Tan box as R-OW; the per-arm calibration keeps the worst-case weighting a valid re-normalisation. \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the dual LP

The same MAX LP as R-OW minus the transport block (no distance matrix). Variables: \(\pi\); \(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0\) (box); a free-sign per-arm calibration multiplier \(\beta_k\).

\[ \max_{\pi,\mu\ge0,\nu\ge0,\ \beta\ \text{free}}\ \sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+\sum_k n\,\beta_k \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped) and the per-unit stationarity

\[ \mu_i-\nu_i\ =\ \tfrac1n\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\beta_{T_i}\qquad\forall i. \]

\(\beta_k\) is the Hájek-calibration dual (free sign — not a Wasserstein radius). Reproduces solve_rw_no_wasserstein_multiarm exactly.

Hajek-OW

Robust worst-case regret over box ∩ Wasserstein (free-π)

An intentional hybrid: Kallus & Zhou's worst-case-regret objective on R-OW's uncertainty set, with a free (non-parametric) policy so a capacity constraint can be enforced.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \max_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\ \big[\,V(\pi_0,W)-V(\pi,W)\,\big],\qquad \pi_0=\text{all-control},\ \ V(\pi,W)=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_i W_iY_i\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — identical to R-OW's (box ∩ Wasserstein):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=\Big\{\,W:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \underbrace{\mathcal W_{D}\!\big(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_k,\,\mathbb{P}^{W}_k\big)\le\varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k}_{\text{(W) Wasserstein covariate balance}}\,\Big\} \]
\[ a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\qquad b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1),\qquad D_{ij}=\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert. \]

The same adversary set as R-OW — only the objective differs (worst-case regret vs \(\pi_0\), not worst-case value). \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ per-arm capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the MIN dual LP

Dualising the inner \(\max_W\) of the regret turns the min–max into a single MIN linear program (objective \(\le 0\); \(\pi_0\) is feasible ⇒ do-no-harm). Variables: \(\pi\); \(\beta_k\ge0\); \(\gamma_{k,i},\theta_{k,j}\); \(p_i,q_i\ge0\) (the regret-box duals, saved as mu/nu).

\[ \min_{\pi,\beta\ge0,\gamma,\theta,p\ge0,q\ge0}\ \sum_k\Big(\beta_k\varepsilon_k+\tfrac1n\sum_i\gamma_{k,i}\Big)+\sum_i\big(b_i p_i-a_i q_i\big) \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped) and

\[ \begin{aligned} &p_i-q_i-\tfrac1n\theta_{k,i}+\tfrac1n\pi_k(x_i)Y_i\ =\ \tfrac1n\mathbf 1[k=0]Y_i &&\forall k,\ \forall i\in I_k\\[2pt] &\beta_k D_{ij}+\gamma_{k,i}+\theta_{k,j}\ \ge\ 0 &&\forall k,\ \forall i,\ \forall j\in I_k \end{aligned} \]

The RHS \(\tfrac1n\mathbf 1[k=0]Y_i\) is \(\pi_0\)'s factual reward — what makes this regret, not value. maximize negates \(Y\) for the loss convention. Reproduces solve_kallus_w_multiarm.

Regret-O

Robust worst-case regret over the box only (free-π)

Hajek-OW without the Wasserstein term — the box-only worst-case-regret minimiser.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \max_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma)}\ \big[\,V(\pi_0,W)-V(\pi,W)\,\big],\qquad \pi_0=\text{all-control}. \]

Uncertainty set — the box only (no Wasserstein term):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma)=\Big\{\,W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}},\ \ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\ \ \forall k}_{\text{per-arm Hájek calibration}}\,\Big\},\quad a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\ b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1). \]

The same box ∩ calibration set as R-O; only the objective differs (worst-case regret vs \(\pi_0\)). \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the MIN dual LP

The same MIN LP minus the transport block. Variables: \(\pi\); \(p_i,q_i\ge0\) (box, saved as mu/nu); free-sign \(\beta_k\).

\[ \min_{\pi,p\ge0,q\ge0,\ \beta\ \text{free}}\ \sum_i\big(b_i p_i-a_i q_i\big)+\sum_k n\,\beta_k \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped) and the per-unit regret coupling

\[ p_i-q_i+\beta_{T_i}+\tfrac1n\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\ =\ \tfrac1n\mathbf 1[T_i=0]\,Y_i\qquad\forall i. \]

Objective \(\le 0\) (do-no-harm). Reproduces solve_kallus_multiarm (\(\pi_0=\) all-control).

Kallus

Parametric softmax regret-minimiser

The faithful Kallus & Zhou method: a softmax policy \(\pi_\theta\) trained to minimise worst-case regret. Flat — no discretisation, no capacity.

In-sample problem

\[ \min_\theta\ \max_{W\in\mathcal U}\ \big[\,V(\pi_0,W)-V(\pi_\theta,W)\,\big],\qquad \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)=\mathrm{softmax}\big(\theta^\top\varphi(x)\big). \]

where the (\(W\)-weighted) IPW value of a policy is \(V(\pi,W)=\tfrac1n\sum_i W_iY_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\), and the baseline \(\pi_0\) assigns arm \(0\) (control) to everyone (\(\pi_{0,0}\equiv1\)) — so its value is just the \(W\)-weighted control outcomes:

\[ V(\pi_0,W)=\tfrac1n\sum_i W_iY_i\,\pi_{0,T_i}(x_i)=\tfrac1n\sum_{i:\,T_i=0} W_i\,Y_i. \]

Uncertainty set — the classic odds set by default (wasserstein=False):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma)=\Big\{\,W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}:\ a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i,\ \ \textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\ \ \forall k\,\Big\},\quad a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\ b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1). \]

With wasserstein=True the set becomes the box ∩ per-arm Wasserstein balls \(\{\mathcal W_D(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_k,\mathbb{P}^{W}_k)\le\varepsilon_k\}\) (R-OW's set). The only structural difference from Regret-O/Hajek-OW is that the policy here is the parametric softmax \(\pi_\theta\), not a free \(\pi\).

How it is solved (Algorithm 1)

For fixed \(\theta\) the inner \(\max_W\) is a Gurobi LP — the same box (or box∩Wasserstein) dual as Regret-O / Hajek-OW. The outer is subgradient descent with the exact policy gradient \(\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(T_i|x_i)=\pi_{T_i}(\text{onehot}-\pi)\varphi(x_i)\), returning the Polyak iterate-average \(\bar\theta=\tfrac1{n_{\text{iter}}}\sum_t\theta_t\); the lowest-regret \(\bar\theta\) across restarts is kept. Deploys with predict_kallus (no Shapley/KNN).

IPW

Hájek / inverse-propensity value maximiser

The non-robust nominal optimum at the estimated weights — no sensitivity model.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i \hat w_i\,Y_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — none: the value is taken at the single nominal weighting \(W=\hat w\) (equivalently \(\mathcal U=\{\hat w\}\), the \(\Gamma=1\) limit of the box where \(a_i=b_i=\hat w_i\)).

How it is solved

A direct LP (no \(\Gamma\), no box, no duals). It is exactly R-O / Regret-O at \(\Gamma=1\).

DoublyRobust

Robust AIPW value maximiser (box-only)

An outcome-model direct term plus a worst-case box bound on the IPW correction of the residual \(r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\).

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)\,\hat\mu_k(x_i)\ +\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma)}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i W_i\,r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i),\qquad r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — the same box ∩ calibration as R-O, applied to the residual \(r\):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma)=\Big\{\,W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}:\ a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i,\ \ \textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\ \ \forall k\,\Big\},\quad a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\ b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1). \]

Only the IPW correction of the residual is made robust; the direct outcome term \(\hat\mu\) is not. Box-only — the box ∩ Wasserstein variant is R-OW-DoublyRobust (and this method = R-O-DoublyRobust). \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the dual LP

R-O's box dual with \(Y\to r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\), plus the (dual-free) direct term added to the objective. Variables: \(\pi\); \(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0\); free-sign \(\beta_k\).

\[ \max_{\pi,\mu\ge0,\nu\ge0,\ \beta\ \text{free}}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k\pi_k(x_i)\hat\mu_k(x_i)+\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+\sum_k n\,\beta_k \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped) and \( \mu_i-\nu_i=\tfrac1n\,r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\beta_{T_i}\ \ \forall i. \)

Reduces to R-O's dual when \(\hat\mu\equiv0\) (bit-identical) and to ordinary AIPW at \(\Gamma=1\).

R-OW-DoublyRobust

Robust AIPW value maximiser (box ∩ Wasserstein)

An outcome-model direct term plus a worst-case box ∩ Wasserstein (the OW set) bound on the IPW correction of the residual \(r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\) — R-OW's covariate-balance robustness with doubly-robust efficiency.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)\,\hat\mu_k(x_i)\ +\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i W_i\,r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i),\qquad r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — the same box ∩ Wasserstein as R-OW, applied to the residual \(r\):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=\Big\{\,W:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \underbrace{\mathcal W_{D}\!\big(\hat{\mathbb P}_k,\,\mathbb P^{W}_k\big)\le\varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k}_{\text{(W) Wasserstein covariate balance}}\,\Big\} \]
\[ a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\qquad b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1),\qquad D_{ij}=\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert. \]

Only the IPW correction of the residual is made robust; the direct outcome term \(\hat\mu\) is not. \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the dual LP

R-OW's box + Wasserstein dual with \(Y\to r_i\), plus the (dual-free) direct term added to the objective. Variables: \(\pi\); \(\beta_k\ge0\) (W-radius); \(\gamma_{k,i},\theta_{k,j}\) (transport); \(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0\) (box).

\[ \max_{\pi,\beta\ge0,\gamma,\theta,\mu\ge0,\nu\ge0}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k\pi_k(x_i)\hat\mu_k(x_i)+\sum_k\Big(-\beta_k\varepsilon_k+\tfrac1n\sum_i\gamma_{k,i}\Big)+\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big) \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped), the residual feasibility \(\tfrac1n\pi_k(x_i)r_i+\tfrac1n\theta_{k,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\ge0\) on factual rows, and the transport-metric constraints \(\beta_k D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\ge0\).

Reduces to R-OW's dual when \(\hat\mu\equiv0\) (verified bit-identical) and to ordinary AIPW at \(\Gamma=1\).

Derivation of the dual

Fix \(\pi\). The direct term \(\tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k\pi_k(x_i)\hat\mu_k(x_i)\) is constant in \(W\) and passes through untouched; the work is dualising the inner \(\min_W\) of the residual correction over the OW set. Write \(I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\) for arm \(k\)'s factual units and \(r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\).

  1. Wasserstein balls as a transport LP. Each ball \(\mathcal W_D(\hat{\mathbb P}_k,\mathbb P^{W}_k)\le\varepsilon_k\) is realised by a coupling \(\zeta^{k}_{ij}\ge0\) (\(i\) over all units, \(j\in I_k\)) matching a uniform demand marginal, the \(W\)-induced supply marginal, and the transport budget \(\varepsilon_k\). The inner problem becomes a joint LP in \((W,\zeta)\), with the Lagrange multiplier shown beside each constraint:
    \[ \min_{W,\ \zeta\ge0}\ \frac1n\sum_i r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,W_i\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \begin{cases} a_i\le W_i\le b_i & (\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0)\\[3pt] \textstyle\sum_{j\in I_k}\zeta^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac1n\ \ \forall i,k & (\gamma_{k,i}\ \text{free})\\[3pt] \textstyle\sum_i \zeta^{k}_{ij}=\tfrac{W_j}{n}\ \ \forall j\in I_k & (\theta_{k,j}\ \text{free})\\[3pt] \textstyle\sum_{i,j} D_{ij}\,\zeta^{k}_{ij}\le\varepsilon_k\ \ \forall k & (\beta_k\ge0) \end{cases} \]
  2. Lagrangian. Attaching those multipliers — box \(\mu_i(a_i-W_i)+\nu_i(W_i-b_i)\), demand \(\gamma_{k,i}\big(\tfrac1n-\sum_{j}\zeta^{k}_{ij}\big)\), supply \(\theta_{k,j}\big(\tfrac{W_j}{n}-\sum_i\zeta^{k}_{ij}\big)\), budget \(\beta_k\big(\sum_{ij}D_{ij}\zeta^{k}_{ij}-\varepsilon_k\big)\) — and grouping the \(\zeta\) and \(W\) terms:
    \[ \mathcal L=\sum_{k}\sum_{i,\,j\in I_k}\big(\beta_k D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\big)\,\zeta^{k}_{ij} \;+\;\sum_i\Big(\tfrac1n r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)+\tfrac1n\theta_{T_i,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\Big)W_i \;+\;\underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_{k,i}\gamma_{k,i}-\sum_k\beta_k\varepsilon_k+\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)}_{\text{constant in }W,\zeta}. \]
  3. Minimise out \(\zeta\) and \(W\). For \(\inf_{W,\zeta\ge0}\mathcal L\) to be finite, the coefficient of each \(\zeta^{k}_{ij}\ge0\) must be \(\ge0\), and the coefficient of each (box-freed) \(W_i\) must vanish:
    \[ \beta_k D_{ij}-\gamma_{k,i}-\theta_{k,j}\ge0\ \ (\text{transport metric}),\qquad \tfrac1n r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)+\tfrac1n\theta_{T_i,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i=0\ \ (\text{factual rows}). \]
    The box is bounded and feasible, so strong LP duality holds: \(\min_W=\) the dual maximum (the boxed constant). The solver encodes the factual-row equality as the equivalent tight inequality \(\ \tfrac1n r_i\pi_{T_i}(x_i)+\tfrac1n\theta_{T_i,i}-\mu_i+\nu_i\ge0\ \) — it binds at the optimum, since the \(+a_i\mu_i\) term drives \(\mu_i\) up until the row is active.
  4. Re-attach the direct term and the outer \(\max_\pi\). The boxed constant is the dual value; adding back the direct outcome term and the policy set \(\Pi\) (simplex, ties, capacity — none touching \(\beta,\gamma,\theta,\mu,\nu\)) merges the two maximisations into the single LP stated above. Because \(r_i\) is data, every term is linear in the joint variables \((\pi,\beta,\gamma,\theta,\mu,\nu)\).

R-O-DoublyRobust

Robust AIPW value maximiser (box only)

An outcome-model direct term plus a worst-case box (the O set) bound on the IPW correction of the residual \(r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\). The R-O member of the DoublyRobust pair — identical to the existing DoublyRobust method.

In-sample problem

\[ \pi^\star=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k \pi_k(x_i)\,\hat\mu_k(x_i)\ +\ \min_{W\in\mathcal U(\Gamma)}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i W_i\,r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i),\qquad r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i). \]

Uncertainty set — the same box ∩ calibration as R-O, applied to the residual \(r\):

\[ \mathcal U(\Gamma)=\Big\{\,W\in\mathbb{R}^n_{+}:\ \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i\ \ \forall i}_{\text{(O) odds / MSM box}},\ \ \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i:T_i=k}W_i=n\ \ \forall k}_{\text{per-arm Hájek calibration}}\,\Big\},\quad a_i=1+\Gamma^{-1}(\hat w_i-1),\ b_i=1+\Gamma(\hat w_i-1). \]

Only the IPW correction of the residual is made robust; the direct outcome term \(\hat\mu\) is not. Box-only — no Wasserstein term. \(\Pi\): \(\pi_k(x_i)\ge0,\ \sum_k\pi_k(x_i)=1\) (+ capacity for Capped).

How it is solved — the dual LP

R-O's box dual with \(Y\to r_i\), plus the (dual-free) direct term added to the objective. Variables: \(\pi\); \(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0\); free-sign \(\beta_k\).

\[ \max_{\pi,\mu\ge0,\nu\ge0,\ \beta\ \text{free}}\ \tfrac1n\sum_i\sum_k\pi_k(x_i)\hat\mu_k(x_i)+\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+\sum_k n\,\beta_k \]

subject to a valid policy (+ capacity when Capped) and \( \mu_i-\nu_i=\tfrac1n\,r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\beta_{T_i}\ \ \forall i. \)

Reduces to R-O's dual when \(\hat\mu\equiv0\) (verified bit-identical); equals the existing DoublyRobust method; ordinary AIPW at \(\Gamma=1\).

Derivation of the dual

Fix \(\pi\). The direct term is constant in \(W\) and passes through; only the inner \(\min_W \tfrac1n\sum_i r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,W_i\) over the O set (box \(\cap\) per-arm Hájek calibration) is dualised. Write \(I_k=\{i:T_i=k\}\) and \(r_i=Y_i-\hat\mu_{T_i}(x_i)\).

  1. Inner LP in \(W\) (Lagrange multiplier beside each constraint):
    \[ \min_{W}\ \frac1n\sum_i r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)\,W_i\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \underbrace{a_i\le W_i\le b_i}_{(\mu_i,\nu_i\ge0)}\ \ \forall i,\qquad \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i\in I_k}W_i=n}_{(\beta_k\ \text{free})}\ \ \forall k. \]
  2. Lagrangian. With box \(\mu_i(a_i-W_i)+\nu_i(W_i-b_i)\) and calibration \(\beta_k\big(n-\sum_{i\in I_k}W_i\big)\), collect the \(W_i\) terms:
    \[ \mathcal L=\sum_i\Big(\tfrac1n r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\mu_i+\nu_i-\beta_{T_i}\Big)W_i \;+\;\underbrace{\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+n\sum_k\beta_k}_{\text{constant in }W}. \]
  3. Stationarity in \(W\). Each (box-freed) \(W_i\) is free, so its coefficient must vanish — giving the dual-feasibility equality and leaving the constant as the dual value:
    \[ \mu_i-\nu_i=\tfrac1n r_i\,\pi_{T_i}(x_i)-\beta_{T_i}\quad\forall i,\qquad \min_W=\sum_i\big(a_i\mu_i-b_i\nu_i\big)+n\sum_k\beta_k. \]
    The box is bounded and feasible \(\Rightarrow\) strong duality, so this maximum equals \(\min_W\).
  4. Single LP. Re-attaching the direct outcome term and the outer \(\max_\pi\) over \(\Pi\) (simplex, ties, capacity) merges into the one LP stated above — linear in \((\pi,\mu,\nu,\beta)\), since \(r_i\) is data.

Rules & conventions

  • Matched Γ \(=e^{\gamma_{\text{true}}/2}\) is always sampled in the sweep (the operating point).
  • Validation: cap length must equal n_arms; method names ∈ the known set; metric ∈ {euclidean}.
  • Capability gating: geometry (zscore/metric) reaches only R-OW & Hajek-OW; maximize reaches only Regret-O, Hajek-OW, Kallus; other methods ignore knobs they don't accept.
  • obj_kind: each saved objective is tagged (worst-case value / regret / robust-AIPW / clairvoyant) — not cross-comparable.
  • Clean guard: re-running an experiment name prunes stale method/variant/Γ outputs left by a prior, different config.
  • No true propensities: weights are always estimated from \((X,T)\) — never \(e_{\text{true}}\)/\(S\).
  • The GUI (application/, Streamlit) lets you set all of this on its Config page; n_arms is sourced from the DGP page so the two can't disagree.

2-arm · 1-D

One discrete feature — R-OW still wins

Data-generating process.
\[ \begin{aligned} X &\sim \mathrm{Unif}\ \text{on the 21-point grid in }[-1,1],\qquad S \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)\ \ \text{(unobserved)},\\[3pt] Y^{0}\mid X,S &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(0.5)\big) \qquad\text{(control: flat, clean)},\\[3pt] Y^{1}\mid X,S &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(-1.2 + 4\,X + 5\,S)\big) \quad\text{(treatment: }X\text{-benefit }+\ S\text{-inflated)},\\[3pt] \operatorname{logit}\Pr(T{=}1\mid X,S) &= -\,X + \gamma\,(S-\tfrac12), \qquad Y = Y^{T},\\[3pt] \gamma_{\mathrm{true}} &= 5 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{matched } \Gamma = e^{\gamma/2}=e^{2.5}\approx 12.18 . \end{aligned} \]

The 1-D reduction of the 2-D experiment (drop \(X_2\), so \(\operatorname{proj}(X)=X\)). \(S\) is the unobserved confounder — it inflates both the treated outcome (\(+5S\)) and the treatment propensity (\(+\gamma(S-\tfrac12)\)), and the assignment is mis-targeted (treats where \(X\) is low, yet treatment truly helps where \(X\) is high).

Ground-truth outcome surfaces

Before any method sees data, here is the true generative outcome model — the success probabilities the DGP actually draws from. This is the signal the robust methods must recover through the fog of the unobserved \(S\).

Expected outcome of each arm as a function of \(X\), averaged over the unobserved \(S\): control (grey) is flat at \(\sigma(0.5)\approx0.62\); treatment (red) rises from \(0.23\) to \(0.97\) and overtakes control near \(X\approx0\) — so the optimal arm is control for \(X\lesssim0\) and treatment for \(X\gtrsim0\). The pink band is the treated outcome's full range over \(S\in\{0,1\}\): at any \(X\) the unobserved confounder can slide the treated success probability across the whole band, which is exactly the ambiguity the sensitivity parameter \(\Gamma\) is meant to absorb.

Ground-truth success probability \(P(Y^k{=}1\mid X,S)\) for every \((X,S)\) pair — one heatmap per arm. Arm 0 (control) is uniformly \(0.62\): no \(X\) or \(S\) channel, so it is never confounded. Arm 1 (treatment) climbs with \(X\) (the \(+4X\) term) and is sharply lifted by the unobserved \(S\) (the \(+5S\) term) — the \(S{=}1\) row (\(0.65\!\to\!1.00\)) sits far above the \(S{=}0\) row (\(0.01\!\to\!0.88\)). Because \(S\) is hidden, two units at the same \(X\) can have very different treated outcomes; that gap is the confounding R-OW hedges against.

A single discrete feature \(X\), one unobserved \(S\), two treatments — the simplest possible setting. With a loose capacity cap the Wasserstein covariate-balance term still binds, so R-OW beats the box-only R-O and the rest. Adding the doubly-robust family lifts the winner further: R-OW-DoublyRobust is the single best method, reaching the best-deployable ceiling. This shows the Wasserstein edge does not need multi-dimensional covariates — only that the capacity constraint binds — and that an estimated outcome-model anchor compounds it.

Why these results happen — the mechanism

One causal chain. The unobserved \(S\) drives both the outcome (treatment success \(=\sigma(-1.2+4X+\mathbf{5S})\)) and the assignment (\(\operatorname{logit}P(T{=}1)=-X+\mathbf{5}(S-\tfrac12)\)). So high-\(S\) units — exactly the ones treatment helps most — are treated more, which makes the observed treated outcome look better than the true effect. A method that trusts the data (IPW) over-treats; one that models the outcome (AIPW) or hedges the weights (R-OW) escapes the trap. Reproduction of Kallus & Zhou (2021) — first synthetic experiment (Fig. 1)

A faithful reproduction of the first synthetic experiment from Kallus & Zhou, “Minimax-Optimal Policy Learning Under Unobserved Confounding” (Management Science 2021), §7.1.1 “Binary Treatments” — the out-of-sample policy regret vs log Γ of Figure 1. The data-generating process and the confounding-robust logistic policy (CRLogit, Algorithm 1) are ported directly from the authors’ released code (paper/kallus/repo/{data_scenarios,subgrad,unconfoundedness_fns}.py).

Data-generating process (verbatim from the repo; the paper’s text has a typo, writing \((2T{-}1)\mu_x\) for the covariate mean where the code uses the unobserved \((2U{-}1)\mu_x\)).
\[ \begin{aligned} U &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)\ \text{(unobserved)},\qquad X\mid U \sim \mathcal N\!\big((2U-1)\,\mu_x,\ I_5\big),\qquad \tilde x=[X,\,1],\\[3pt] Y(t) &= 2.5\,t + \beta^\top\tilde x + t\,\beta_{\text{treat}}^\top\tilde x + \alpha\,U\,(2t-1) + \omega\,U + \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,4),\\[3pt] \tilde e(x) &= \sigma(\beta_{\mathrm{TC}}^\top\tilde x)\ \ \text{(nominal propensity)},\qquad U^\star=\mathbb 1[\,Y(1)\lt Y(0)\,]\ \ \text{(treatment lowers risk)},\\[3pt] e(x,U) &= \text{odds-tilt of }\tilde e\text{ by } e^{\pm\log\Gamma_*}\text{ toward }U^\star,\ \ \log\Gamma_*=1.5, \qquad T\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\big(e(x,U)\big),\quad Y=Y(T). \end{aligned} \]

Parameters: \(\mu_x=[-1,.5,-1,0,-1]\), \(\beta=[0,.5,-.5,0,0,0]\), \(\beta_{\text{treat}}=[-1.5,1,-1.5,1,.5,0]\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{TC}}=[0,.75,-.5,0,-1,0]\), \(\alpha=-2\), \(\omega=1.5\). \(Y\) is a risk/loss (lower is better). The confounder \(U\) shifts both the covariates and the outcome, and units that benefit from treatment (\(U^\star{=}1\)) are assigned it more often — “doctors treat those who benefit, on factors not in the data.” The true confounding strength is \(\log\Gamma_*=1.5\).

Finding (reproduces the paper’s central message). The naive IPW logistic policy is harmed by the confounding — it over-treats and realises positive regret (\(+0.26\pm0.04\), worse than never-treating). CRLogit (the confounding-robust logistic) starts at the IPW value as \(\log\Gamma\to0\), then its realised regret dips below zero, bottoming near the true \(\log\Gamma_*=1.5\) (min \(\approx-0.20\) at \(\log\Gamma\approx1.3\)), and rises back to \(0\) for over-large \(\Gamma\) (it conservatively returns the never-treat baseline once it cannot certify improvement) — exactly the shape of Figure 1. Robustness calibrated near the true confounding level recovers a genuinely improving policy where IPW does harm. Oracle ceiling (treat iff true CATE lowers risk): \(-0.88\).

Data-generating process (Kallus & Zhou 2021, §7.1.1; ported verbatim from the authors’ code). The outcome \(Y\) is a risk / loss — lower is better; \(\sigma\) is the logistic sigmoid, \(I_5\) the \(5\times5\) identity, and \(\tilde x=(X,1)^\top\) the covariate augmented with an intercept.
\[ \begin{aligned} U &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12) && \text{(unobserved confounder)},\\[5pt] X \mid U &\sim \mathcal N\!\big((2U-1)\,\mu_x,\ I_5\big) && \text{(5-D covariates; mean flips sign with }U\text{)},\\[5pt] Y(t) &= 2.5\,t \;+\; \beta^\top \tilde x \;+\; t\,\beta_{\text{treat}}^\top \tilde x \;+\; \alpha\,U\,(2t-1) \;+\; \omega\,U \;+\; \varepsilon, \quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,4) && \text{(potential risk, }t\in\{0,1\}\text{)},\\[5pt] U^\star &= \mathbb{1}\big[\,Y(1)\lt Y(0)\,\big] && \text{(}=1\text{ iff treatment lowers risk)},\\[5pt] \tilde e(x) &= \sigma\!\big(\beta_{\mathrm{TC}}^\top \tilde x\big) && \text{(nominal / unconfounded propensity)},\\[5pt] T \mid X,U &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\big(e(X,U)\big),\qquad Y = Y(T) && \text{(assignment and observed outcome)}. \end{aligned} \]
The true (confounded) propensity \(e(x,U)\) tilts the nominal odds by \(e^{\pm\Lambda}\) toward the patient-optimal arm — units that benefit are over-treated — with strength \(\Lambda=\log\Gamma_\ast=1.5\):
\[ e(x,U)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{e^{\Lambda}\,\tilde e(x)}{\,1-\tilde e(x)+e^{\Lambda}\,\tilde e(x)\,}, & U^\star=1 \quad (\text{benefits} \Rightarrow \text{treated more}),\\[12pt] \dfrac{e^{-\Lambda}\,\tilde e(x)}{\,1-\tilde e(x)+e^{-\Lambda}\,\tilde e(x)\,}, & U^\star=0 \quad (\text{harmed} \Rightarrow \text{treated less}). \end{cases} \]

Is CRLogit (the paper) the same as our Kallus? Same algorithm (Kallus & Zhou Algorithm 1 — a parametric softmax/logistic regret-minimiser by subgradient descent), but not bit-identical: (i) uncertainty set — the repo’s CRLogit uses the odds-ratio tilt (get_bnds, axis \(=\log\Gamma\)), our fit_kallus uses Tan’s marginal-sensitivity box (marginal_sensitivity_box, axis \(=\Gamma\)); (ii) inner worst-case — repo is a closed-form self-normalised sort, ours a Gurobi LP with per-arm Hájek calibration; (iii) weights — repo uses the nominal \(\sigma(\beta_{\mathrm{TC}}x)\), ours estimates \(\hat P(T\mid x)\). So the curves match in shape, not in numbers.

(2) The two doubly-robust methods break the degeneracy. (3) Parametric Kallus is still best — treats 0.59 beneficial vs 0.14 harmful, test regret \(-0.53\) (\(\approx60\%\) of the gap), and uniquely trends down with Γ overall (\(-0.35\) at \(\Gamma{=}1\) to \(-0.62\) at \(\Gamma{=}9\), non-monotonically). Why the IPW methods collapse. With no capacity and continuous \(X\) (every training point its own cell), the uncapped IPW value/regret optimum is a per-point rule — “keep the observed arm if its weighted outcome was good” — identical for every box/Wasserstein variant and, deployed by NN, uninformative about benefit. Robustness only bites when points are coupled (a capacity constraint, or discrete cells with many units each — the 1-D/2-D experiments). The doubly-robust direct term sidesteps this by injecting a cross-unit signal (the fitted \(\hat\mu\)); a fully discriminating policy still needs the parametric class of Kallus & Zhou.

A DGP where our methods beat Kallus, AIPW and IPW

A deliberately simple, explainable 1-D 2-arm DGP built so the free-π Wasserstein methods (R-OW, R-OW-DR) beat all three baselines — the parametric Kallus, the doubly-robust AIPW, and plain IPW. Two ingredients do it: the optimal policy is non-monotone (treat a middle subgroup), and the unobserved confounder both biases the control outcome and over-treats good-responders concentrated at the harmful edges.

Data-generating process. One discrete feature \(X\) on a 21-point grid in \([-1,1]\); a Bernoulli confounder \(S\) is unobserved. \(\sigma\) is the logistic sigmoid.
\[ \begin{aligned} S &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12) && \text{(unobserved confounder)},\\[3pt] Y(0) &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12))\big) && \text{(control DEPENDS on the unobserved }S\text{; marginal still }\tfrac12\text{)},\\[3pt] Y(1) &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(7(0.36-X^2)+5(S-\tfrac12))\big) && \text{(treatment HELPS a middle band }|X|\lesssim0.5\text{, HURTS the edges)},\\[3pt] T \mid X,S &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12)(1+5X^2))\big) && \text{(high-}S\text{ units treated more, concentrated at the EDGES)}. \end{aligned} \]
So the true optimal policy is "treat the middle band"; and at the edges — where treatment truly hurts — the treated units are a high-\(S\) selected elite whose observed outcomes look fine. Because the control outcome is also driven by \(S\), both arms' observed means are biased by the same confounder — so the naive outcome model \(\hat\mu\) is pulled the wrong way on each side. Why each baseline fails, and we win:
  • Kallus — its logistic policy \(\sigma(\theta^\top\tilde x)\) is monotone in \(X\); it cannot represent "treat a middle band" ⇒ it collapses to ≈never-treat.
  • IPW — confounded weights + the edge over-treatment make the harmful edges look good ⇒ mis-targets.
  • AIPW — its outcome model \(\hat\mu\) is a monotone logistic in \(X\) (it can't bend to the bump) and its residual correction is non-robust ⇒ misled by the edge confounding.
  • R-OW / R-OW-DR — free-π (can represent any band) \(+\) the Wasserstein covariate-balance term flags the edge-imbalanced treated distribution \(+\) the MSM box hedges the confounding ⇒ they treat the right middle band. The Wasserstein term is the differentiator: R-OW \(\gt\) R-O.

Uncapped

The uncapped version — why R-OW "treats everyone", and why the cap matters

The previous tab gives the free-π methods a 50% capacity budget that the parametric Kallus structurally cannot honor — an unfair edge. Here we run the exact same non-monotone DGP with no cap (cap=(1.0,1.0)), so every method, Kallus included, is on equal footing. The honest result: uncapped, the robust value methods (R-OW, R-O) treat ≈ everyone and land on the never-treat floor; no method recovers the beneficial band. This tab explains why — it isn't R-OW being naive, it's what the confounded data genuinely shows.

Setup. Identical to the non-monotone tab (\(X\) on a 21-pt grid; unobserved \(S\sim\)Bernoulli(½); control \(Y(0)\sim\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12))\); treatment \(Y(1)\sim\sigma(7(0.36-X^2)+5(S-\tfrac12))\); assignment \(T\sim\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12)(1+5X^2))\)) — only the cap is removed. \(n=500\) train / 2000 test, matched \(\Gamma\approx12.18\), 8 seeds.

Step 1 — the selection: who actually gets treated

The assignment \(T\sim\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12)(1+5X^2))\) means motivated units (\(S{=}1\)) are treated far more, and the gap widens at the edges. At \(|X|\to1\), \(S{=}1\) is treated with probability \(\approx1\) and \(S{=}0\) with probability \(\approx0\) — a near positivity violation. So the treated group is essentially all \(S{=}1\) and the control group essentially all \(S{=}0\): the methods can never compare like with like.

Step 2 — what the methods see (the data lies)

Because the control group is an unrepresentative low-\(S\) subset, the observed control mean (\(\approx0.1\), grey) sits far below the true control (0.50, dashed). The result: the observed data shows treated ≳ control everywhere — including the edges, where the truth is the opposite (true control 0.50 ≫ true treat 0.06, so treatment hurts). Concretely at \(X{=}1\): observed treated \(=0.12\), observed control \(=0.076\) ⇒ "treat looks better", while the true marginals say control \(0.50\) vs treat \(0.06\). The methods have no evidence anywhere that treating is bad.

Why R-OW treats everyone (the crux). R-OW maximizes the worst case of the value it can estimate from this confounded data — not the true effect. Three things combine: (1) the observed data says treat ≳ control in every cell; (2) the robustness box deflates both arms together, so "treat ≳ control" survives the worst case; (3) robustness can re-weight the data it has but cannot conjure the missing \(S{=}1\)-control outcomes (they don't exist — positivity violation). So R-OW sees no reason to withhold treatment anywhere, and with no budget forcing a choice it treats everyone. The harmful edges then cancel the good middle, and realised value collapses to ≈ never-treat. Plain IPW and AIPW are fooled the very same way.

Result — uncapped, nobody recovers the band

The Wasserstein edge is gone too (R-OW = R-O exactly). Uncapped, there is simply no clear winner.

The punchline — why the cap matters. The capacity cap in the previous tab is not a trick: it is the missing ingredient that turns a per-cell yes/no into "spend a limited budget on the best cells." Forced to rank, R-OW's robust worst-case correctly puts the genuinely-beneficial middle band above the look-alike edges (where the data is least trustworthy), so it targets the band and beats the naive methods. The budget supplies the pressure to choose — and that is exactly where robustness pays off. Removing the cap to be fair to Kallus also removes that pressure, so the differentiation collapses for everyone. (A fairer route than dropping the cap is to make Kallus capacity-aware — e.g. cap its top-scored 50% — keeping the meaningful comparison.)

Real data · LaLonde

LaLonde / NSW job-training — recovering a benefit under strong hidden confounding

This experiment is built on the famous LaLonde / National Supported Work (NSW) job-training data — the canonical real-data stress test for causal methods. We use it to manufacture a realistic unobserved confounder: we train the policies on a strongly biased observational pairing while hiding the covariates (prior earnings) that drive the bias, and then we grade every learned policy against the randomised experiment, where the true treatment effect is known.

What the data is

The National Supported Work demonstration was a 1970s program that gave disadvantaged workers (ex-offenders, long-term unemployed, former drug users) a temporary subsidised job. Two data files are used:

  • 8 covariates: age, education (years of schooling), black, hispanic, married, nodegree (no high-school diploma), re74 and re75 — where re74 / re75 are real earnings in 1974 / 1975 ($), i.e. pre-treatment income.
  • Outcome: re78 — real earnings in 1978 ($), measured after the program. We rescale it to $1000s throughout.

The ground truth: the experimental ATE

Because nsw_dw.dta is randomised, the program's true average effect on 1978 earnings is just the treated mean minus the control mean: \[ \widehat{\text{ATE}} \;=\; \overline{re78}_{\,treated} - \overline{re78}_{\,control} \;=\; 6349.14 - 4554.80 \;\approx\; +\$1{,}794 . \] So the program genuinely helped: trainees earned about $1,794 more in 1978 (≈ +1.79 in $1000-units). The benefit is not uniform — it rises with education (the per-bin experimental effect goes from about −$251 at ≤8 years up to about +$7,150 at ≥13 years), which is exactly the signal a good policy could exploit.

How the unobserved confounder is created — the heart of it

We do not train on the clean experiment. Instead we build the observational LaLonde pairing: keep the real 185 NSW trainees as the treated group, but replace the experimental controls with PSID survey people as the "controls". Each seed draws 420 PSID controls (sampled without replacement), giving a training set of \[ \underbrace{185}_{\text{NSW trainees (treat=1)}} \;+\; \underbrace{420}_{\text{PSID controls (treat=0)}} \;=\; 605 \text{ rows.} \] This pairing is severely confounded because the two groups are not comparable: trainees were selected because they were disadvantaged, while PSID controls are ordinary earners. The decisive imbalance is in the prior-earnings columns:

Now the trick. We show the methods only ONE covariate — education (binned) — and hide the other seven (age, black, hispanic, married, nodegree, and crucially re74, re75). Each method estimates the propensity \( P(T\mid\text{education}) \) and the outcome model from education alone. The hidden prior-earnings — which simultaneously drive who is treated (selection) and how much they earn in 1978 (outcome) — therefore act as a genuine, ≈10× unobserved confounder. With earnings hidden, the data "looks like" treatment is catastrophically harmful: the naive treated-vs-PSID earnings difference is about −$15,205, the opposite sign of the true +$1,794. This is the famous LaLonde reversal, and it is exactly the regime our robustness machinery is meant to handle.

Evaluation: deploy on the randomised experiment

Observational data has no counterfactuals, so it cannot grade a policy honestly. Instead we take each learned per-bin policy \( \pi(\text{treat}\mid\text{education}) \) and deploy it on the randomised experimental NSW (all 445 units), estimating its true value by inverse-propensity weighting: \[ \widehat{V}(\pi) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\pi(T_i\mid \text{edu}_i)\, Y_i}{P(T_i)}, \qquad P(T{=}1)=\tfrac{185}{445}\approx 0.4157 . \] Because treatment was randomised, the propensity \( P(T) \) is known exactly (it does not depend on any covariate), so this estimate is unbiased — no modelling assumptions needed. This is what makes the NSW experiment a trustworthy scoreboard for policies that were learned on confounded data.

The confounding and the famous bias it creates, visualised

The hidden prior earnings differ ~10× between the trainees (\(\approx\$1.5\text{–}2.1\)k) and the PSID controls (\(\approx\$19\)k): the trainees are disadvantaged, the controls are not — the textbook unobserved-confounding structure.

With prior earnings hidden, the naive observational comparison says the program loses ≈ $15,200 per person; the randomised experiment says it gains ≈ $1,794. Hiding the confounders turns a beneficial program into a catastrophically harmful-looking one.

The confounding also mis-targets the treatment

At \(\Gamma=3\), IPW treats the 9- and 11-year education bins but skips the ≥13-year bin where the true benefit is largest (\(+\$7{,}150\), right panel). In the observational data the high-education trainees are compared against high-earning PSID controls, so high-education treatment looks worst — the opposite of the truth. Confounding doesn't just suppress treatment; it inverts the targeting.

What this shows about the robust framework — it does the right thing, just not a flashy thing.
  • Robustness is conservative, not corrective. When confounding makes a good action look bad (the LaLonde direction), the MSM box can only hedge toward the safe never-treat option — it cannot manufacture the positive effect the data hides. The designed DGPs are the opposite direction — a bad action dressed up as good — which is where hedging actively wins.
  • The Γ-sweep makes the uncertainty explicit. As Γ grows the methods back away from treating; a sensitivity analysis says you would need a Γ on the order of the induced 10× odds (astronomically large) to call the observational "don't treat" robustly justified — exactly the honest verdict for LaLonde.
  • A confounded outcome model can hurt. The DR methods anchored to the poisoned \(\hat\mu\) refuse everything, while the model-free IPW-value methods at least capture a sliver — a caution about doubly-robust corrections under strong unobserved confounding.

Real data · IHDP

IHDP — validating on real covariates with known counterfactuals

This experiment stress-tests the policy-learning methods on real infant-health covariates rather than a hand-built simulation. We use the IHDP benchmark: real covariates from a randomised early-childhood study, paired with a simulated outcome surface so that — uniquely — both potential outcomes are known and we can measure each learned policy's true realised value. We then deliberately hide all but four binary covariates — pushing every continuous feature and most of the binary ones into the unobserved-confounder set.

What IHDP is

The Infant Health and Development Program (IHDP) was a randomised controlled trial of intensive specialist support — high-quality childcare and home visits by a trained specialist — for low-birth-weight, premature infants. Here it is used in its now-standard form as a semi-synthetic benchmark (the Hill 2011 recipe, which also underlies the ACIC-style causal-inference competitions): the covariates are real, but the response surface is simulated. Concretely, a researcher generates two known outcome functions \(\mu_0(x)\) and \(\mu_1(x)\) — the expected outcome under control and under the program — and removes a non-random subset of treated infants to manufacture confounding.

Because the outcome surface is synthetic, the two potential outcomes mu0 and mu1 are known for every infant (both the factual and the counterfactual). That is the whole point: on real RCT data you only ever see one of the two, but here we can score a policy by the outcome it would actually have produced, and compare against a true oracle.

The data we actually load (verified)

We use the public CEVAE/NPCI replications, files ihdp_1.csv … ihdp_10.csv (10 replications). Each file has 747 infants and 30 columns with no header, in the order treat, yf, ycf, mu0, mu1, x1 … x25:

  • treat — the home-visit / childcare program indicator. 139 infants are treated (18.61%); the other 608 are controls. This split is identical across all 10 replications (the real covariates and treatment are shared — only the outcome surface is re-simulated).
  • yf — the factual outcome; ycf the counterfactual.
  • mu0, mu1 — the known potential outcomes under control and under treatment. We confirmed both columns are present and use them as ground truth in evaluation.
  • x1 … x25 — 25 infant covariates: we measured 6 continuous (x1–x6) and 19 binary (x7–x25).

From the known outcomes we verified the true treatment effect. The true ATE \(=\operatorname{mean}(\mu_1-\mu_0)\) is about 4.0 in replication 1 (it ranges roughly 3.9–10.5 across the 10 reps, because the outcome surface is re-simulated each time). Crucially, the effect is positive for almost every infant: the fraction with CATE \(=\mu_1-\mu_0\gt 0\) is ≈99% (99.2% in rep 1). In other words, the program helps virtually everyone, so the genuinely optimal policy is "treat almost all infants" — a point that matters when reading the results.

Why IHDP, and the ACIC substitution

Our intended real-covariate benchmark was the ACIC-2016 competition data ("real covariates + synthetic-but-known outcomes"), but the raw ACIC-2016 files were unavailable (the download returns a 404). IHDP is the canonical ACIC-style benchmark — same philosophy of real covariates with a known simulated response surface — so we use it as the stand-in. If real ACIC files are supplied, the identical pipeline can be rerun on them unchanged.

How we create the unobserved confounder

The methods are given a deliberately impoverished view of each infant. Of the 25 covariates we keep only four binary features — x7, x9, x14, x21 — the four (of the 19 binary covariates) most predictive of the treatment effect. Because they are 0/1, no binning is needed: every infant collapses to a 4-bit cell (one of at most \(2^4=16\) types), and a learned policy is one treatment probability per cell.

Everything else is HIDDEN: all 6 continuous covariates x1–x6 — including x6, the single strongest driver of the effect, which the earlier version of this study had observed — plus 15 of the 19 binary covariates, for 21 unobserved confounders in total. They still drive the outcomes \(\mu_0,\mu_1\), but the methods never see them, so they act as the unobserved confounders that the robustness parameter \(\Gamma\) is meant to guard against.

Honest caveat: hiding the strong drivers makes the effect harder to target, but IHDP's treatment is still only weakly correlated with the covariates. The confounding-strength diagnostic over the hidden set, \(\max_{j\in\text{hidden}}\lvert\operatorname{corr}(T,x_j)\rvert\), is just ≈ 0.15 (the most treatment-correlated covariate, x25, sits in the hidden set). So the unobserved confounding remains mild; this benchmark validates that the methods run correctly on real covariates, while the sharp method separation lives in the strongly-confounded studies.

Protocol & evaluation

  • No capacity cap. Each arm may be assigned to up to 100% of units (cap \(=(1.0,1.0)\)). We run uncapped so the comparison is fair to the parametric Kallus method, which cannot enforce a capacity constraint.
  • Train / deploy split. Each replication is randomly split (seeded by the rep number) into 60% train / 40% held-out test — that is 448 train and 299 test infants. Policies are learned on the training split's observed 4-bit cells.
  • Realised-value scoring. Each learned policy is deployed by 4-bit cell (each test infant matched to its observed-feature cell) to the 40% held-out test split, and its realised value is scored with the known potential outcomes: for each test infant the policy's treat/control probabilities are weighted against that infant's \((\mu_0,\mu_1)\) and averaged. This is the policy's true expected outcome — only possible because the counterfactuals are known.
  • 10 replications serve as 10 seeds (mean ± SD), at robustness level \(\Gamma=2\).
  • Scale-free metric. The raw outcome scale varies enormously across reps (per-rep means span roughly 8 to 50), so a raw average is misleading. We instead report the fraction of the oracle gain captured, \[\frac{\text{value}-\text{ctrl}}{\text{oracle}-\text{ctrl}}\in[0,1],\] where ctrl \(=\operatorname{mean}(\mu_0)\) is the never-treat (control) value and oracle \(=\operatorname{mean}(\max(\mu_0,\mu_1))\) treats each infant optimally. 0% means "no better than never-treating", 100% means "matched the oracle". This is scale-free and comparable across reps.

Sensitivity to Γ — sweeping the assumed confounding strength

We re-ran the whole experiment across \(\Gamma\in\{1,2,3,4,6,8\}\) (10 reps each). Four methods respond to \(\Gamma\) (R-OW, R-O, R-OW-DR, Kallus); IPW and AIPW are non-robust references that ignore \(\Gamma\).

Semi-synthetic

Semi-synthetic LaLonde — a setting where R-OW is the best method

On the real LaLonde outcomes, R-OW cannot come out on top — the confounding there is "benign-direction" (training truly helps, the data just makes it look harmful), which is exactly the regime where robustness is a cost, not an advantage. To show R-OW where it is designed to win, we keep LaLonde's real covariates but simulate the outcome with the structure robustness is built for — a harmful subgroup whose treated units are made to look good by a hidden confounder. This is the standard semi-synthetic recipe (real covariates + a known, simulated response surface) behind IHDP and the ACIC competitions; we are fully transparent that only the outcome is synthetic.

Why this experiment exists — the recipe R-OW needs. A worst-case-robust method wins only when the confounding makes a harmful action look good: the naive methods take the bait and treat units they shouldn't, while R-OW hedges and avoids the loss. That needs (1) a subgroup where treatment is genuinely harmful, (2) a hidden confounder that positively selects good-prognosis units into treatment in that harmful region, (3) an observed covariate that exposes the resulting distributional imbalance, and (4) a binding capacity cap. Real LaLonde supplies 2–4 but not 1 (training helps essentially everyone), so we build 1 in by simulation.
How the data is built (the construction, in full).
  • Covariate = real age. We pool the real ages of the LaLonde experimental NSW (445) and the PSID controls (2490) and quantile-bin them to a 21-point grid \(x\in[-1,1]\) (\(x=-1\) youngest, \(x=+1\) oldest). The age distribution is real; nothing else about a person is used.
  • Hidden confounder \(S\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12)\) — a latent "motivation / ability", never observed by any method.
  • Simulated outcome — a success indicator (think: employed / earned above a threshold in 1978), with \(\sigma\) the logistic sigmoid:
    \[ \begin{aligned} Y(0) &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12))\big) && \text{(control: motivation helps; flat in age)},\\[3pt] Y(1) &\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(7(0.36-x^2)+5(S-\tfrac12))\big) && \text{(training HELPS prime-age }|x|\lesssim0.6\text{, HURTS the young/old edges)}. \end{aligned} \]
    The treatment effect is therefore non-monotone in age: positive for a prime-age band, negative at both edges — the "lost-wages" story (training costs time, and at the edges that cost outweighs the benefit).
  • Confounded assignment — motivated workers are treated more, and that over-selection is concentrated at the edges, exactly where training hurts:
    \[ T \mid x,S \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\!\big(\sigma(5(S-\tfrac12)(1+5x^2))\big). \]
    So at the harmful edges the treated are a high-motivation elite whose observed success looks fine — which is what fools IPW and AIPW.
  • What each method sees: only age (\(x\)); \(S\) is hidden. matched \(\Gamma=e^{\gamma/2}=e^{2.5}\approx12.18\); capacity cap \((1.0,0.5)\) (control uncapped, treat \(\le\)50%); \(n=800\) train / 2000 test; 3 seeds.

Who gets treated

The crux: R-OW / R-OW-DR concentrate treatment on the prime-age band (the truly-helped subgroup, green); Kallus is a flat logistic that cannot carve it out; IPW and AIPW scatter treatment toward the confounded edges, where training actually hurts.

Why each method lands where it does.
  • Kallus — its logistic policy \(\sigma(\theta^\top\tilde x)\) is monotone in age, so it cannot represent "treat a middle band" and collapses to ≈never-treat.
  • IPW / AIPW — the edge-concentrated selection makes the harmful edges look beneficial; IPW's confounded weights and AIPW's monotone \(\hat\mu\) are fooled into treating them.
  • R-OW / R-OW-DR — free-π (any age-band) \(+\) the Wasserstein covariate-balance term flags the edge-imbalanced treated age-distribution \(+\) the MSM box hedges the confounding ⇒ they treat the right prime-age band. The Wasserstein term is what separates R-OW from box-only R-O.
