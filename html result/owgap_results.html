0. Problem setup, methods, and protocol

We learn an individualized treatment rule π(X) ∈ [0,1] from observational data (Xi, Ti, Yi) whose treatment assignment depended on an unobserved confounder S, maximizing the policy value

V(π)=𝔼[π(X)Y(1)+(1−π(X))Y(0)],π*=\argmaxπV(π)

Because e(X,S) is not identified from (X,T,Y), plug-in ("naive") methods carry hidden-confounding bias. The robust methods optimize the worst case over an uncertainty set of inverse-propensity weights: the marginal sensitivity model (odds-box, "O") bounds how far the true propensity odds may deviate from the fitted observable propensity,

Γ−1≤e(X,S)/(1−e(X,S))e^(X)/(1−e^(X))≤Γ

and the Wasserstein covariate-balance constraint ("W") additionally requires the worst-case reweighted treated and control covariate distributions to stay within transport cost ϵ of each other (ϵ set data-driven as cϵ times the tightest feasible value):

𝒲(∑iwi(1)δXi,∑iwi(0)δXi)≤ε

Evaluation protocol

Matched Γ. Every DGP has a known true selection odds ratio Λ; headline numbers are quoted at Γ = Λ (no tuning), with full Γ-curves showing misspecification behavior. Value. Discrete experiments report exact (noise-free) policy value on the level grid; continuous experiments report realized test value on fresh draws with known potential outcomes, deploying support policies by KNN. Uncertainty. Mean ± SD over seeds, plus paired per-seed 95% CIs for the headline margins. Ablations. Coupling strength α (when does balance help), transport budget cϵ, capacity budget (30/40/50%), and the Lipschitz constant L (continuous).

1. The data-generating process (base owgap)

Seven discrete fitness levels X uniform in [-1,1]; unobserved vitality S; aggressive-therapy treatment T; outcome Y (higher is better). N=600 per seed unless stated.

P(S=+1∣X)=σ(10X),e(X,S)=clip(σ(0.8S−2X),0.02,0.98)
μ0=8S,μ1=9S+1.5X,Y(t)=μt+𝒩(0,0.62)
CATE(X)=(2σ(10X)−1)+1.5X⇒oracle treats iff X>0

S shifts both arms by ±8–9 while the treatment differential is ~1–2.5: treated patients look great because they are vital, not because therapy works. The true selection odds ratio is Λ=4.95, so Γ=5 is the matched sensitivity level — results quoted "at matched Γ" involve no tuning. Reference values: never-treat 0, all-treat 0, oracle 0.847.

Where the confounding bias lives (a design feature, stated up front). Selection on S operates at every X (the 0.8S term in the propensity), but it can only BIAS a within-X comparison where X fails to pin down S — i.e. where Var(S|X) = 4p(1-p) with p = σ(αX) is non-negligible. At the strong coupling α=10 this is the σ(αX)≈½ region: on the 7-level grid, the X=0 level (naive CATE inflation +6.5; faint traces ±0.8 at X=±1/3); in the continuous variant, a band |X|≲0.2 that shifts the naive decision threshold. This concentration is the unavoidable consequence of ANY monotone X–S coupling crossing one-half, not a planted artifact; the continuous experiment shows the band version of the same effect, and the α sweep (Discrete tab, Section 4) shows what happens as the region widens.

2. The v2 redesign: making the showcase decisive (in flight)

Diagnosis from Sections 2–5: the naive methods' signature error is treating X=0 — the one level where S is genuinely uncertain given X, so within-level selection inflates the estimated CATE from ~0 to ~+6.5. In the base DGP that error is costless (true CATE(0)=0), which mutes the uncapped margin and lets the cap rescue naive methods. exp_owgap_v2 keeps the whole structure and makes exactly that error expensive:

μ1=9S+3X−1⇒CATE(X)=(2σ(10X)−1)+3X−1,CATE(0)=−1

plus a genuinely scarce budget: cap 30% < the 43% oracle-treat mass. Unchanged: coupling, propensity (so Λ=4.95 and matched Γ=5), oracle = treat iff X>0, uncapped oracle 0.847, never-treat 0. New: all-treat = -1 (over-treatment is now visibly bad).

2. External baseline: Kallus & Zhou (box-only, parametric)

The confounding-robust softmax policy learner of Kallus & Zhou, run with the marginal sensitivity box only (20 seeds, uncapped — the smooth policy class cannot enforce a hard capacity). It peaks at 0.32 near Γ=1 and collapses to the never-treat value ≈0 by Γ≈2.5 — at the matched Γ=5 it does nothing, while IPW-O-W holds 0.757. The same collapse occurs at every coupling strength in the α sweep. The box alone forces total pessimism; only the Wasserstein balance constraint lets robustness coexist with a non-trivial policy.

1. Continuous X and the Lipschitz constant L

Continuous version (X ~ Uniform(-1,1), same DGP; N=400 train, KNN deployment to 2000 test units, 3 seeds). With per-unit policies (L=∞) every support point is its own parameter: the policy overfits, pooled mass vanishes and Γ is inert. Two dials fix it: bin/smooth the policy (the Lipschitz class |π(x)-π(x')| ≤ L|x-x'|) for the VARIANCE, and Γ robustness for the BIAS. The L × Γ surface shows both.

Moderate smoothing (L≈1.5–3) is the sweet spot: tight enough to restore pooling so Γ bites, loose enough to keep the policy's decision boundary sharp.

Real data: UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals (69,990 patients)

Covariate X = composite frailty score (age, medications, length of stay, labs, procedures), rescaled to [-1,1]; hidden confounder S = acuity (prior inpatient/ER visits, diagnoses, facility discharge), median-split; treatment T = the real insulin decision (53% treated). The REAL X–S coupling is weak: corr(X,S)=0.196, matched Γ=1.26 — by the coupling-sweep diagnostic (Discrete tab, Section 4) this dataset sits OUTSIDE the O-W operating regime. We therefore run two complementary experiments rather than forcing a win:

Experiment A — in-regime semi-synthetic (real covariates, simulated confounding). Real bootstrapped X (real covariate geometry); S ~ Bern(σ(12(X-0.07))) calibrated to the real acuity rate 0.249 with E[Var(S|X)]=0.23 (comparable to the owgap α=10 regime); T synthetic with the real fitted X-coefficient, real treated fraction 0.51, and selection strength CS=0.8 giving Λ=4.95 — the SAME matched-Γ=5 protocol as owgap. Outcomes: μ0=-3S, μ1=-1.5S+X-0.3 (sicker patients do worse in both arms; insulin helps the sicker and frailer, minus a fixed burden). The failure mode is the mirror image of owgap: sicker patients get insulin AND do worse, so the naive plug-in concludes insulin is harmful and treats NOBODY (naive = never-treat 1.51 vs oracle 1.78, oracle treats the frailest 21%). Together the two experiments show both classic confounding failures — over-treatment (owgap: hidden vitality flatters the drug) and under-treatment (here: hidden acuity damns it) — each corrected by the same O-W machinery.
Experiment B — fully real (X, S, T): the diagnostic validation. Everything real except the calibrated synthetic potential outcomes (needed for ground truth). With the real weak coupling the linear-naive gap is only 0.001 (naive ≈ oracle) and the matched Γ is 1.26: the scoping diagnostic PREDICTS robustness is unnecessary here. Running the full method suite and observing exactly that — naive fine, O-W at matched Γ harmless, large Γ over-hedging — validates the diagnostic on real data and shows the method does no harm outside its regime.

Experiment A results (in-regime)

Oracle 1.763, never-treat 1.491, all-treat 0.268, naive DR 1.504. Best overall: DoublyRobust-O-W at Γ=3, L=3 → 1.702. Kallus best 1.471.

Verdict A: prediction confirmed. The naive plug-in (1.504) sits essentially at never-treat (1.491) — it concluded insulin is harmful and treated almost nobody, the classic under-treatment failure. The robust methods recover ~75% of the naive-to-oracle gap (IPW-O-W 1.697, DR-O-W 1.702 at L=3, Γ=3–4; margin +0.19 over naive), while the parametric box-only Kallus baseline lands BELOW never-treat (1.471). On real covariate geometry with in-regime confounding, the correction works in the under-treatment direction just as it does in owgap's over-treatment direction. Box-only O-X variants perform close to O-W here (1.69) at their best Γ — the Wasserstein term's decisive advantages remain the discrete capped regime and stability across Γ (see the other tabs); on this axis the honest claim is "all Γ-robust methods fix the failure; O-W is never worse".

Experiment B results (fully real)

Oracle 1.284, never-treat 1.248, all-treat 0.804, naive DR 1.268. Best overall: IPW-O-X at Γ=1, L=2 → 1.263. Kallus best 1.231.

Verdict B: the diagnostic passes on real data. With the real weak coupling, naive DR (1.268) is already near-oracle (1.284) — a gap of 0.016. Every robust method's optimum sits at Γ=1 (≈ the real matched Γ=1.26) with value 1.263 ≈ naive: robustness at the matched level does NO harm. Raising Γ strictly degrades value (1.258 → 1.201 at L=3) — the over-hedging the coupling diagnostic predicts out-of-regime. Together with the α sweep this closes the loop: the measurable coupling diagnostic tells practitioners when the O-W machinery pays off, and real data behaves exactly as it forecasts.
